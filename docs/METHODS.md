# Methods: formulas and data used by SoilGuard

This file lists every calculation the app makes, with its source. `docs/RESEARCH.md` holds the practice-by-practice evidence behind the recommendations. The same tables are in the app's Methods sheet (page icon on the map).

## 0. Data sources

**Kept for 15 days.** So that a place looked at again loads at once, the answers that stay the same for days are kept in the browser (Cache Storage) for 15 days: Soil Data Access, USGS and Open-Meteo elevation, NLCD and Sentinel-2 land cover, TIGERweb county lines, NASA POWER, Open-Meteo's archive, ACIS (PRISM), NCEI GHCNh and IEM station records, gridMET's past days and the place search. Never kept: live map layers and outlooks, this week's Drought Monitor, and gridMET's day count (its newest day). A POST (Soil Data Access, ACIS) is kept under its address and a hash of its request. Older answers are dropped at the next visit; clearing the site's data in the browser drops them all. The Drought Monitor's past weeks at a point are kept in browser storage for the last 60 locations, as they never change; the dated Drought Monitor maps the area grid reads are kept for 15 days like the rest. Two results the app works out itself sit in the same box under keys of their own and with their own limits: a gridMET Drought cell's values (6 hours) and the layout of gridMET's files on its file server (while the file's Last-Modified holds); which gridMET server answered last is kept in localStorage (§8, *Which gridMET server* and *Kept between visits*). The Soil maps' services are asked fresh each visit.

**Pacing, never a quota.** Every request the page sends itself (`fetchJSON`, `fetchText`, `fetchBlob` and `fetchQ` in `js/util.js`; the byte-range readers, the NLCD WCS, and the National Water Model and USCRN files use `fetchQ`) waits for a place in its server's line: at most 6 are open to one host at the same instant, and the rest go first come, first served. A request holds its place from when it is sent until its answer starts (the headers), not while a long answer downloads; its time limits count from when it is sent, never while it waits; and a request no longer needed (the point moved) leaves the line unsent. There is no count per visit, per minute or per day: a visitor can search as much and as long as they like, and a busy moment only spaces their requests out. With 50 to 75 visitors at once, this keeps each page from opening dozens of queries to one small server (Soil Data Access, The National Map) at the same moment. The app's own files skip the line (GitHub Pages' CDN is not the bottleneck), and Leaflet's map tiles and WMS images are image loads, which the browser paces itself.

**Asking again.** A request is sent again, at most twice, after HTTP 429 or 503 (busy), after 502 or 504 (a gateway's error), or when it did not get through (fetch's network error, which is also how an error page without a CORS header looks to the page). The last two are asked again only when the failure came within 8 s: a later one is most likely a gateway that has already waited its own limit (on 12 Nov 2026 an NHDPlus HR query failed after 10 s), and two more tries would triple the wait for an error. The waits are about 1 s and 3 s, each times a random 0.5–1.5 so that pages turned away at the same moment do not return together, or the server's Retry-After where the page may read it (0.5 to 20 s); the place in line is let go while waiting. Never after another 4xx or a 500 (the same question gets the same answer), a stop by the page, or the request's own time limit; each try has its full time limits. POSTs are asked again too, as they are read-only queries (Soil Data Access, ACIS, The National Map). The Iowa Environmental Mesonet reader also asks again twice after a time-out, as before. A server's error is shown only after the last try.

**Daily check of the data services.** `tools/check_services.py`, run by `.github/workflows/data-services.yml` every day at about 11:17 UTC, or by hand (**Actions › Data services check › Run workflow**, or `python tools/check_services.py` with `requests` installed), sends 37 small requests of the kinds the pages make, at 31.044, −97.347 (cropland in Bell County, Texas): soil (the Soil Data Access query and soil map WMS, the USFS soil image services, USGS mrdata, ISRIC SoilGrids REST and WMS), land and terrain (MRLC NLCD WMS and WCS, Esri Sentinel-2 land cover, USGS 3DEP and Open-Meteo elevation, TIGERweb, USA Structures, USA Detailed Water Bodies), water (WBD, NHDPlus HR, NLDI), weather and climate (Open-Meteo forecast and archive, NASA POWER, ACIS, NCEI GHCNh and USCRN, the Iowa Environmental Mesonet, gridMET THREDDS and its file server), drought (NDMC WMS, the Living Atlas Drought Intensity layer, NIDIS tiles), soil moisture and live maps (NASA GIBS, NOAA map services, the National Water Model files on AWS, Crop-CASMA, NRCS AWDB), and place search and base maps (Nominatim, Esri World Imagery, The National Map). Each is sent with the site's Origin, 8 at a time with a 30 s limit (under 2 minutes in all), and passes when the status is right, the answer looks right (a key, a type or its first bytes) and, for a service the page reads with fetch(), Access-Control-Allow-Origin allows the site (for a POST, its CORS preflight too). A busy answer (429, 502, 503, 504) or a dropped connection is asked once more after 5 s. The table (service, what was asked, result, time, note) goes into the run's summary. When any check fails, the workflow opens the issue **Data services check: failing** with the table, or comments the new table on the one already open; on the first run where all answer, it comments "All services answered" and closes the issue. Services known to fail at times (gridMET THREDDS, NHDPlus HR, WBD, ISRIC SoilGrids REST, USGS 3DEP, NCEI GHCNh, IEM, Crop-CASMA) are marked "known flaky" in the note but still count. The script exits 1 when any check fails. The repository is public, so the issue is public too (it names only the outside services and their answers), and GitHub turns off a public repository's scheduled workflows after 60 days without a commit; the daily run then needs turning back on under Actions.

## 1. Bare-soil wind-erosion potential (Overview risk card, statewide map)

The app computes Wind Erosion Equation (WEQ) step E3 for a bare, smooth (K = 1), wide, unsheltered field:

**E = I × C / 100** (t/ac/yr)

- **I**, soil erodibility index, comes from SSURGO `component.weg` / `component.wei` (NSSH Part 618, §618.93). When an edit to the surface layer changes what the NSSH texture rules give, the app uses the rules' group (§2); otherwise it keeps the survey's.
- **C**, climatic factor, is the official NRCS value from the SSURGO "Climate Factor" land-area overlap for the map unit (weighted by the map unit's acres where it lies in several, or their plain mean where SSURGO gives no acres). In a county SSURGO gives no usable value, it is the county's estimate, the same as on the map (County C, below). Texas examples: Dawson 70, Lubbock 70, Potter 90, Gaines 80, Ward 130, El Paso 130, Harris 10.
  - The definition is C = 34.483 v³ / PE² (Chepil et al. 1962). v is the mean annual wind in mph at 30 ft. PE is the Thornthwaite P–E index, Σ 115 [P/(T−10)]^(10/9), with P in inches (minimum 0.5) and T in °F (minimum 28.4). Garden City, KS = 100.
- **Erodibility index** = C·I/T. T is the SSURGO tolerable soil loss. A value ≥ 8 meets the USDA highly-erodible-land wind criterion (7 CFR 12.21), with C applied as a fraction.

**Risk classes** (t/ac/yr):

| Low | Moderate | High | Very high | Extreme |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| < 10 | 10–40 | 40–80 | 80–150 | ≥ 150 |

The class edges correspond to roughly 2, 8, 16 and 30 times T = 5. They are app conventions, not NRCS classes. The risk card shows all five classes under the headline with the site's class lit, and says that E is an amount (t/ac/yr), not a score. The exact E is classed in every display unit; where rounding would carry a value across an edge it is shown with one more decimal (39.56 t/ac/yr reads 39.6, Moderate, not 40). In t/ha/yr the edges read 22.4, 90, 179.3 and 336.3: an edge label is a whole number unless rounding would lower it.

**Scales of the other numbers on screen** (each is shown next to its value):

| Number | Scale | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Wind Erodibility Group (WEG) | 1, 2, 3, 4, 4L, 5, 6, 7, 8 | 1 is the most erodible, 8 is not erodible; 3, 4 and 4L share I = 86, and 4L holds calcareous (limy) loamy soils (§2) |
| Wind erodibility index I | 0–310 t/ac/yr | set by the WEG: the yearly loss from a wide, bare, smooth field in the climate of Garden City, KS |
| Climatic factor C | % of Garden City, KS (= 100); official county values 1–300 (the desert Southwest highest), Texas 4–150 | higher is windier or drier |
| Tolerable soil loss T | 1–5 t/ac/yr | the most a soil can lose each year without losing productivity |
| Erodibility index EI | 0 and up; ≥ 8 is highly erodible land | C·I/T |
| Erosive wind hours | 0–8,760 h/yr | hours with 10 m wind at or above the threshold |

**Statewide map** (`tools/build_layers.py`): SoilWeb's 800 m WEI grid, which is derived from SSURGO, multiplied by the county's C (or the survey area's, below). The result is reprojected to Web Mercator at 1 km resolution and clipped to the state (`python tools/build_layers.py all`, the 48 contiguous states and DC). Each state's images, statistics and county C table go in its own file, loaded only when the state is chosen.

**County C** (`tools/fetch_county_c.py`, `county_table` in `tools/build_layers.py`). SSURGO links each map unit to the counties and the WEQ climate-factor areas (whose symbol is C) it lies in, with its acres in each county. A county's C is the mean of its areas' symbols weighted by acres: each map unit's acres in the county are split between its climate-factor areas in proportion to its acres in each (equally where SSURGO gives none), and where SSURGO leaves a symbol's acres empty the symbols are weighted by map units instead. Where a county's soil survey areas use different climate-factor areas (63 counties, mostly in the West: Arizona, Idaho, Montana, Washington), each survey area's part of the county takes its own mean, drawn with the survey-area outlines from SSURGO (`sapolygon`, simplified to about 200 m), and the county's own value is those means weighted by each survey area's extent in the county (SSURGO gives no acres for whole survey areas in a few, Park County, MT among them); inside a survey area the climate-factor areas have no outlines, so the map shows their mean there. Two kinds of symbol are read as "not rated": 0 (Alabama, Arkansas, Georgia, Louisiana, North Carolina and Virginia give 0 to every county they list; a place with wind has C above 0) and 100 in Michigan (14 of the 15 Upper Peninsula counties carry it, and Baraga has no climate-factor area: the Garden City reference itself, against 5–6 in the Wisconsin counties across the border and single digits from the formula). That leaves 1,518 of the 3,109 contiguous counties rated: none in 16 states (AL, AR, DC, DE, GA, KY, LA, MD, ME, MS, NC, OH, TN, VA, VT, WV) and some counties without in 19 more (most of California, Colorado, Illinois, Minnesota, Nevada, New Mexico, New York, Oregon, Utah and Wyoming; about half of Washington; Michigan's Upper Peninsula and Ionia County; five counties each in Massachusetts and Pennsylvania; one in Arizona, Florida, Iowa, Montana and New Hampshire).

County codes: the map uses the 2010 Census counties and SSURGO its own county symbols. Shannon County, SD (46113) is SSURGO's Oglala Lakota (46102). St. Louis city and eight Virginia cities have no SSURGO county: their soils are in the surrounding county's survey (St. Louis County; Rockbridge, Rockingham, Prince William, Wise, Montgomery), checked at points in each, so they take that county's C; Galax straddles Carroll's and Grayson's surveys, and the map gives it Carroll's (both are estimates in the same class). Three SSURGO codes have no 2010 county (Yellowstone National Park's Montana part, Clifton Forge and South Boston, now towns) and copy the county now holding them.

A county without a rating takes an estimate: Chepil's C = 34.483 v³ / PE² (§1 equations) from the NASA POWER 2001–2025 climatology (MERRA-2, 0.5° × 0.625°; a grid point outside the contiguous states, over the sea, a Great Lake, Canada or Mexico, takes the value of the nearest point inside them), averaged over the county on a 0.05° lattice, times 1.64, the median ratio of official to formula C over the rated counties (the formula alone runs low). Tested by estimating every rated county with its own state left out (and out of the ratio): the same C class as the official value (the map's 20-wide classes) for 65% of plains counties (median error 28%) and 99% of eastern counties; in the mountain West the estimate runs low, about 2.5 times, because a 0.5° cell's rain falls mostly on the high ground (63% in the same class). Scaling by the nearest rated counties' own ratios did worse in the West (41%) and blew up next to states that give one value statewide (Pennsylvania 8, Connecticut 20), conventions rather than the formula's; spreading the official values themselves (inverse distance) ignores sharp climate edges: it would give Los Angeles County 264 and Santa Barbara 300 from the desert counties, against 38 and 29 from the formula. The legend gives each chosen state's share of area with an estimated C (above six such states, their number).

The point and the area tool read the same table (`tableC` in `js/app.js`). The county is the one at the point (Census 2020 counties from TIGERweb) when the map unit lists it, else the map unit's county with the most acres (St. Louis city and the Virginia cities fall back this way; the Census 2020 counties still have Connecticut's eight old counties, which SSURGO also uses). In a county with an estimate, the estimate (over a map unit's own 0 or Upper Peninsula 100 too). Elsewhere the map unit's own NRCS areas, acre-weighted, which can be finer than the map's county or survey-area mean; else the county's (or its survey area's) value. The area tool finds each point's county from the TIGERweb counties over the area (outlines generalized to about 30 m), uses the same C for its grid, and its C range and "estimated" note count only the points analysed. Small C shows one decimal (estimates go down to 0.1, which must not read as the not-rated 0).

## 2. Wind Erodibility Groups

**NRCS's texture guide to the groups** (National Soil Survey Handbook, Part 618 Subpart B, §618.93, amended August 2024). The Soil tab shows this table with the site's group marked; the Methods sheet shows it in full.

| WEG | Surface layer | I (t/ac/yr) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Very fine sand, fine sand, sand, coarse sand | 160 (coarse sand) to 310 (very fine sand); 220 on average |
| 2 | Loamy very fine sand, loamy fine sand, loamy sand, loamy coarse sand; very fine sandy loam and silt loam with ≤ 5% clay; sapric material (except Folists) | 134 |
| 3 | Very fine sandy loam (not WEG 2), fine sandy loam, sandy loam, coarse sandy loam; noncalcareous silt loam with 20 to < 50% very fine sand and 5 to < 12% clay | 86 |
| 4 | Clay, silty clay; noncalcareous clay loam and silty clay loam with > 35% clay; none with sesquic, parasesquic, ferritic, ferruginous or kaolinitic (iron-rich) mineralogy | 86 |
| 4L | Calcareous loam, silt loam, silt, sandy clay, sandy clay loam, clay loam, silty clay loam | 86 |
| 5 | Noncalcareous loam with < 20% clay; noncalcareous silt loam with 5 to < 20% clay (not WEG 3); noncalcareous sandy clay loam and sandy clay; hemic material | 56 |
| 6 | Noncalcareous loam and silt loam with ≥ 20% clay; noncalcareous clay loam and silty clay loam with ≤ 35% clay; iron-rich silt loam | 48 |
| 7 | Noncalcareous silt; iron-rich noncalcareous silty clay, silty clay loam and clay in Oxisols or Ultisols; fibric material | 38 |
| 8 | Not susceptible to wind erosion because of rock or pararock fragments at the surface and/or wetness; Folists | 0 |

For a named soil, NRCS uses the group recorded in the soil survey; the texture guide is for soils that are not identified (National Agronomy Manual, Part 502: "For most NRCS uses, the I value is assigned for named soils based on wind erodibility groups"). A soil's recorded group can differ from what its texture alone suggests. Pullman clay loam is WEG 7 in 40 of 45 Texas survey records, and Randall clay in playas is WEG 7 too, though by texture they would be 6 and 4. For a soil you have not edited, the app shows the survey's group. It labels a soil's group only by its number and I, never by a texture name; texture names appear only in the class table, as the guide.

**How the app assigns the group from edited values.** The app keeps the survey's group unless your values change what the texture rules below give, so a pH edit leaves Pullman clay loam in WEG 7. The texture class comes from the USDA triangle. The survey's sand-size modifier (e.g. FSL) is kept when the family is unchanged.

| WEG | Surface texture rule | I |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | VFS, FS, S, COS | 310 / 250 / 220 / 160 |
| 2 | Loamy sands; VFSL or SIL with ≤ 5% clay | 134 |
| 3 | Sandy loams; noncalcareous SIL with 20–50% very fine sand and 5–12% clay | 86 |
| 4 | C, SIC; noncalcareous CL/SICL > 35% clay | 86 |
| 4L | Calcareous L, SIL, SI, SC, SCL, CL, SICL | 86 |
| 5 | Noncalcareous L < 20% clay; SIL 5–20% clay; SCL; SC | 56 |
| 6 | Noncalcareous L/SIL ≥ 20% clay; CL/SICL ≤ 35% clay | 48 |
| 7 | Noncalcareous SI | 38 |
| 8 | Through the fragment rule: > 60% rock fragments by volume (not sands or loamy sands), or WEG 6 with ≥ 35% or WEG 7 with ≥ 15% (the next lower I after 48 and 38 is 0) | 0 |

- **RWEQ cross-checks** (Soil tab; Fryrear et al. 1994, 1998):
  - Erodible fraction: EF = (29.09 + 0.31 Sa + 0.17 Si + 0.33 Sa/Cl − 2.59 OM − 0.95 CaCO₃)/100, the fraction of dry aggregates < 0.84 mm.
  - Soil crust factor: SCF = 1/(1 + 0.0066 Cl² + 0.021 OM²).
  - All inputs are in %. Values are flagged when the soil is outside the fitted range: Sa 5.5–93.6, Si 0.5–69.5, Cl 5–39.3, Sa/Cl 1.2–53, OM 0.18–4.79, CaCO₃ 0–25.2.
  - The RWEQ weather factor and transport-capacity equations are listed in the Methods sheet for reference but not computed. They need field length, roughness and cover.
- "Calcareous" in the NSSH means strong or violent effervescence. The app uses CaCO₃ ≥ 5% as a proxy, because the NSSH gives no CaCO₃ threshold.
- Coarse fragments move the group toward less erodible: one group at 15–35% by volume, two groups at 35–60%, and I = 0 above 60%. Sands and loamy sands move fewer groups (none at 15–35%, one at 35–60%, three above 60%). One group means the next lower I (160 → 134, 86 → 56), so 3, 4 and 4L all move to 5.
  - The volume is SSURGO's `fragvoltot_r` when the survey gives it. Otherwise the app takes the whole-soil weight (`frag3to10_r` + `fraggt10_r` for cobbles and stones, plus 100 − % passing the No. 10 sieve for the rest of the soil) and converts it with the layer's bulk density and 2.65 g/cm³ for rock: 17% by weight is about 10% by volume.
  - A texture modifier sets a floor at the middle of its class: gravelly, cobbly, stony, bouldery, channery, flaggy or cherty 15–35%, "very" (e.g. STV) 35–60%, "extremely" (e.g. CBX) 60–90%.
- Not applied, because the survey's own group already covers them for an unedited soil: iron-rich mineralogy, andic material, wetness and Folists.

## 3. Wind climatology at the point (Wind tab)

**Period:** the last 5, 10 or 20 complete calendar years, or your own dates. MERRA-2 runs from 2001 and ECMWF from 1979, both up to a few days ago (about 8 and 3 days). Your own dates need 30 days or more inside the chosen model's record, with 20 or more of them in one calendar month; dates partly outside the record are cut to it, with a message, and fewer than 30 days inside it are refused. A shared or typed link whose dates cannot be analysed says why and shows the last 10 years; dates that only the other model holds switch to it. A day runs midnight to midnight in the location's state's standard time, without daylight saving: UTC−5 Eastern, −6 Central, −7 Mountain, −8 Pacific, the zone most of the state keeps (`tools/build_states.py`; a state split between two zones takes the larger part's, as Texas takes Central for El Paso). The station downloads, NASA POWER's and Open-Meteo's hours and "today" all use it. Months are averaged by weight, so an uneven period does not tilt the year: a month needs 480 hours or more to count, yearly totals are shown only when all 12 months are covered, a calendar year counts as complete at 90% of its hours, and the trend needs 5 complete years. Calibration trains on at least 3 years around the period, inside the model's record (widened forward when the record starts too late). The page address keeps the period (`&from=YYYY-MM-DD&to=YYYY-MM-DD`, or `&period=5` or `20`) and, when the model is not MERRA-2, `&model=ecmwf`, so a shared link redoes the same analysis.

**If the chosen model fails,** the other one analyses the same dates when its record holds them. Otherwise a preset period is worked out again for it (and the airport looked up again), and custom dates are cut to its record; with fewer than 30 days left, or no month with 20 days, the first model's error is shown.

**Data:** for the chosen period:
- **Hourly 10 m wind speed and direction.** You choose the gridded model on the Wind tab, and each model is the other's automatic fallback: when the chosen source returns an error or has not started answering within 20 s (both normally answer in 1–3 s), the app switches; once data is arriving, a slow download is allowed up to 90 s.
  - **NASA MERRA-2 (default):** the NASA POWER hourly API, direct, UTC, at 0.5° × 0.625°. It has no gust variable.
  - **ECMWF (backup or option):** the Open-Meteo archive `best_match`, which is ECMWF IFS analysis at 9 km from 2017 on and ERA5 at 0.25° (~28 km) before 2017. ERA5-Land does not supply wind here. Daily max gust comes with it.
- **Daily precipitation, grass reference ET₀ (ASCE Penman–Monteith) and Tmax/Tmin:** gridMET over OPeNDAP, one 4 km cell. Scale factor, offset and fill value are read from the dataset attributes. The first fallback is NASA POWER daily (direct): precipitation, temperature, dew point, 2 m wind and all-sky solar radiation, from which the app computes FAO-56 Penman–Monteith ET₀ (Allen et al. 1998, Eqs. 6–21 and 37–40; albedo 0.23, G = 0; Hargreaves on days missing an input). The last fallback is Open-Meteo daily precipitation, FAO-56 ET₀ and mean temperature.
- **Daily max wind** comes from the hourly series.
- **Open-Meteo cost:** the default lookup makes no Open-Meteo calls. If you pick ECMWF, a 10-year lookup costs about 130 Open-Meteo "calls" (78 at the site plus 52 at the station), against free limits of 600 per minute and 10,000 per day per IP.

**Calibration to the nearest station (default):**
- **Station data:** routine hourly METARs (FM-15: 2-minute mean wind at 10 m, plus gusts) from NOAA NCEI GHCNh, one request per year. The report nearest each whole hour is kept, and NCEI quality codes 2, 3, 6 and 7 (suspect or erroneous) are dropped. Stations without a GHCNh id, or with GHCNh coverage below the threshold, use the Iowa Environmental Mesonet ASOS archive.
- **Quality control:** sustained wind > 40 m/s or gust > 55 m/s is dropped. Hobbs, for example, has 250-kt garbled reports that otherwise dominate cubic energy sums.
- **Station choice:** the nearest airport within 150 km with ≥ 60% of hours over its training window and over the period. NWS/FAA ASOS sites rank ahead of AWOS by a 100 km handicap, because in West Texas several AWOS anemometers (Seminole, Andrews, Snyder, Pecos) read 40–60% fewer erosive hours than nearby ASOS. You can pick another station in the Wind tab.
- **Method:** monthly empirical quantile mapping. It is trained on the same gridded model at the station's grid cell versus the station on the same hours, using 101 quantiles between 0.1% and 99.9%, with end ratios beyond those. It is then applied to reanalysis at the site, so the model's site-vs-station difference is kept. Daily max gusts are mapped the same way against station daily max gusts; with MERRA-2, which has no gusts, the model's daily max hourly wind is mapped onto station gusts. Direction and timing stay from the model.
- **Other sources you can select:** "Airport station only" (rain and ET₀ still from gridMET) and "Gridded model only".
- **Check, Lubbock 2016–2025:** reanalysis vs LBB mean wind 4.41 vs 5.24 m/s, hourly r = 0.80, RMSE 1.85 m/s. Erosive hours (≥ 8 m/s) were 5.3% vs 15.1%.
  - Calibrated: 1,304 erosive h/yr vs the station's 1,321 (reanalysis 451).
  - High-risk months: Feb–May calibrated and station, Jan–Apr reanalysis.
  - Drift potential: 553 VU calibrated vs 153 VU reanalysis.

With gridMET moisture, the Lubbock calibrated high-risk months are Feb–Apr. May falls just under the cutoff (9.3% of annual risk), because gridMET records more May rain (77 mm) than the ECMWF model did.

**Erosive threshold:** 8 m/s at 10 m by default. This is the NRCS figure of 13 mph at 1 ft, or about 18 mph at 30 ft (Lyles & Krauss 1971; NAM Part 502). You can change it from 5 to 12 m/s.

**Monthly metrics:**
- Erosive hours: hours with u ≥ uₜ.
- Erosive energy: Σ u²(u − uₜ).
- Dryness: D = clamp((ET₀ − P)/ET₀, 0, 1), with P and ET₀ from gridMET. If no moisture data loads, D = 1 and months rank by wind energy alone.
- **Risk index** = energy × D. A month's **share** is its index divided by the annual total.
  - **Red** (high risk): share ≥ 10% (vs. 8.3% if spread evenly).
  - **Amber**: share ≥ 6.7%.
  - These cut-offs are an app convention.
  - Red, amber and high-risk months are given only for a period covering all 12 months. With fewer, the shares are of the months covered, no month is ranked (they show gray), and Practices uses the typical Feb–May windy season, labelled as such.

**Gust days:** daily max gust ≥ 17.9 m/s (40 mph). Shown as n/a for uncalibrated MERRA-2, and for a tower calibration with no airport within 150 km.

**FAO climatic index:** (1/100) Σ u₂³ · D · days. u₂ = 0.748 u₁₀ (FAO-56 Eq. 47). Months with P > ET₀ are clamped to 0.

**Fryberger & Dean (1979) drift potential:** DP = Σ V²(V − 12)/N, with V in knots at 10 m over all hours N.
- RDP is the vector resultant of those terms in the downwind direction; RDD is its direction.
- DP classes: low < 200, intermediate 200–399, high ≥ 400 VU.
- RDP/DP: multidirectional < 0.3, intermediate < 0.8, unidirectional ≥ 0.8.

**Prevailing erosive wind** is the 22.5° sector carrying the most erosive energy Σu²(u − uₜ), smoothed with half weight on its neighbors. Barrier and strip orientation is that direction plus 90°. There is none when no hour reaches the threshold.

**Trend:** ordinary least-squares slope of erosive hours per year, with a two-sided t-test at 95%.

Airport anemometers sit on open, mown ground (mesonet towers should too; the exposure check tests it), so calibrated values describe exposed fields. Sheltered sites blow less.

- Tables: mapunit, legend, sacatalog, muaggatt, component, chorizon, chtexturegrp (RV), corestrictions, coecoclass, cocropyld, coeplants, muaoverlap/laoverlap (MLRA, C, R).
- The "dominant soil" is the largest component that has horizon data.
- The Soil maps theme's Soil tab shows it, from the survey read for the wind erosion (no request of its own): the dominant soil, the Horizons card (Physical and water view; the Chemistry view is §11), the pH box, site and classification, components, yields and plants.
- Wind erosion › Soil keeps what sets the erodibility: the dominant soil's WEG, I and surface texture (§1–2).
- Sand, silt, clay, OM and CaCO₃ are edited on Wind erosion › Soil, and pH in the Soil tab's pH box. The edited layer is the horizon marked ✎, in both Horizons views.
- Wind erosion › Soil's Reset to survey restores its five values and keeps a pH edit. The pH box's Reset undoes every edit.

## 5. Field or area analysis (area tool) and the map-click check

**Boundary:** drawn on the map (tap the corners; tap the first corner, double-click or Finish to close) or uploaded. Zipped shapefiles and loose .shp/.dbf/.prj files are read with shpjs, which reprojects using the .prj; KML and KMZ with togeojson (and JSZip for KMZ); GeoJSON directly. Everything is read in the browser. Every polygon in the file counts; lines and points are ignored. No size limit: boundaries over about 500 km² (123,550 ac) are read in 20 km tiles, a few at a time, and the time grows with the area; the panel shows progress and says which share of the area, if any, a service did not answer for. Over 500 km² at most 150,000 building footprints, 120,000 road pieces and 60,000 water features are read; the share of the area past those counts is named in the panel and its points are not left out for that layer. Area is the spherical polygon area (WGS84 radius).

**Soil polygons:** one Soil Data Access query clips the SSURGO map-unit polygons to the boundary (`mupolygongeo.STIntersection(boundary)`, with the boundary simplified to at most 600 vertices). Two more queries read each map unit's components (WEG, I, %) and its climatic-factor areas and counties, with acres; TIGERweb's Census 2020 counties give each point its county.

**Left out** (when "Leave out buildings, roads and water" is ticked):
- **Buildings:** FEMA/ORNL USA Structures footprints.
- **Roads:** Census TIGER primary, secondary and local roads, as a band around the centreline. Half-widths by MTFCC class: S1100 15 m, S1200 9 m, S1400 5 m, S1630 6 m, S1640 4 m, S1730/S1740/S1750 3 m, S1500 2 m, paths 1.5 m, others 4 m.
- **Water:** USGS NHD water bodies of type lake/pond (390), reservoir (436), swamp/marsh (466) and estuary (493). Playas (361) stay in because most are farmed. Esri's copy of NHD is asked at the same time and used if NHD has not answered within 5 s. SSURGO map units named "Water" are always left out.
- Points outside the chosen states or without soil data.

**Shares:** about 20,000 stratified random points (on areas over 500 km², more as the area grows, up to 70,000; one per grid cell, seeded so the same area gives the same answer) are tested against the boundary, the left-out features and the soil polygons. Each kept point adds each component's percentage (÷ 100) to that component's WEG and to its risk class, with E = I × C / 100, where C is the point's `tableC` value (§1, County C): the county estimate where the county has one, else the map unit's NRCS C, else the county's (or its survey area's). Shares are of the open land in rated components; components without a WEG (rock outcrop, urban land, pits) are reported separately. Acres = share × analysed acres. The sampling error of a 20,000-point share is under ±0.7 percentage points.

**Checks:** a 157 ac field uploaded as GeoJSON, KML, KMZ, a zipped WGS84 shapefile, a zipped UTM 14N shapefile and loose UTM .shp/.dbf/.prj files gave 158 ac every time. A 2 km square on the edge of Shallowater: 826 ac, 77 ac of roads and 76 ac of buildings left out, 66% WEG 6. A 100 km² square in south Lubbock took 6 s.

**Area grid** (`js/grid.js`, `js/gridfiles.js`): the boundary as a grid of cells, with soil and weather in each.
- *Cells:* from 20 cm up. Square, north up or turned by an angle clockwise; hexagons with points or flat sides up, sized by their width across the flats; or your own grid of polygons (cells whose centres are inside the boundary are kept, with your id column). Rows and columns are whole steps from an origin: 0, 0 by default, so grids of one size, angle and origin line up, turned or not; or a corner of a grid you have, as X and Y.
- *Coordinates, layout:* the cells are true squares or hexagons in the system they are laid out in: UTM in the area's zone (the default) or a zone picked from 1–23 within 30° of longitude of the area, on WGS 84 (EPSG:326zz, the default) or NAD83 (EPSG:269zz); CONUS Albers (EPSG:5070); or Web Mercator (EPSG:3857). The sheet and README give the system's scale east–west and north–south at the area's centre (from steps of 0.0001° on the ellipsoid) when it is off by more than 0.1%: UTM within its own zone never is (0.9996–1.0007 in the lower 48), Albers keeps areas, so its squares are a little wider than tall or the reverse on the ground (0.7% each way at Lubbock), and a ground metre is about 1/cos(latitude) of Web Mercator's metres (1.20 at Lubbock). Its cells are therefore made larger by the scale at the area's centre, so they have their size on the ground there, or, if asked, are sized in its own metres to match web map tiles (only then do grids of one size line up from one area to another, as the scale changes with latitude); road half-widths and the cell counts use the same scale.
- *Coordinates, files:* the shapefile and the x, y of the CSV are in the layout's system (the default), WGS 84 or NAD83 longitude and latitude (EPSG:4326, 4269), Web Mercator, or a system given by a proj4 definition the user pastes (`+proj=` longlat, utm, tmerc, aea, lcc, merc or webmerc; the PROJ ellipsoids, or +a with +b, +rf or +f, or +R; any +units or +to_meter; a 3 or 7 parameter +towgs84 shift, position vector as PROJ; a grid shift, +nadgrids or NAD27, other prime meridians and axis orders are refused with the reason). Every corner and cell centre goes from the layout to longitude and latitude and on into that system, with a point at least every 100 m along an edge: a straight 1 km edge in UTM bows up to 26 mm into degrees and 23 mm into Web Mercator in the lower 48 (15 and 13 mm at Lubbock), and the points keep it within 0.3 mm. Your own grid's corners come from its file's longitudes and latitudes. The shapefile's .prj is ESRI WKT: for the systems built in, the text PROJ 9.5 writes for the code (checked string for string with pyproj, and read back as the same EPSG code); for a pasted definition, written from its parameters (false easting and northing in its unit, as ESRI wants), or the user's own .prj (WKT) written as given. An ESRI .prj cannot hold a +towgs84 shift, and the sheet and README say so. GeoJSON is always WGS 84 longitude and latitude (RFC 7946). The GeoTIFF stays in the layout's system, since a raster cannot be reprojected without resampling its cells; its GeoKeys are those GDAL writes (projected, pixel is area, a citation with the system's name, GeographicTypeGeoKey 4326 or 4269, degrees, ProjectedCSTypeGeoKey the layout's code, metres), and GDAL 3.10 reads them back as that code. README.txt names each system with its EPSG code; `crs.txt` gives each one's proj4 definition and WKT; the GeoTIFF also carries it as LAYOUT_CRS. NAD83 and WGS 84 longitudes and latitudes are taken as the same, as EPSG:1188 (NAD83 to WGS 84 (1), a null shift good to 4 m) does; they differ by 1–2 m in the lower 48.
- *Projections:* worked out in the app (`js/gridfiles.js`), as there is no PROJ library in the page: transverse Mercator by the Krüger series (Karney 2011) with latitude from the conformal latitude by iteration, Albers, Lambert conformal conic (one or two parallels) and Mercator (Snyder 1987). Checked against pyproj (PROJ 9.5) at 12 random points in each of 24 systems (the built-in ones; State Plane Texas Central in US feet and Arizona Central in feet; Lambert-93; British National Grid with its 7-parameter shift; World Mercator; UTM south; Texas Centric Albers; a one-parallel Lambert; spheres): forward within 0.07 mm, the rounding of the reference, and back within 0.01 µm (1.4 mm through a 7-parameter shift, which is taken at height 0 each way, as PROJ does for 2D points). A +proj=lcc with two parallels and no +lat_0 has its origin on the equator, as in PROJ.
- *How much detail:* finer cells line up with other grids but add no information. The soil survey is mapped at 1:12,000–1:24,000, a map unit mixes soils without showing where each lies, and the weather comes in cells of 4 km or more. The sheet and README say so.
- *A cell's value:* what lies at its centre (polygons are painted on the lattice row by row, so 50 million cells are possible), or weighted by what covers it, from 25 points in the cell's box, up to 200,000 cells; there a code (map unit, Wind Erodibility Group, weather cell) is the value covering the most, counted by value, as map units can share a group, and every weather column is that one weather cell's. Your own grid is always weighted. Under the centre rule each weather cell is painted with its sides along parallels and meridians (a point every 0.002°) and cells on its edges are looked up by their centre, so every cell takes the weather cell its centre lies in.
- *Soil:* SSURGO components and horizons of the map units in the boundary (Soil Data Access), on the layer bottoms you choose: weighted means (organic matter and CaCO₃ by mass) and harmonic Ksat, as in §7, but a layer below the end of a profile is left blank rather than repeated. Within a map unit, the main soil that has the value, or all its soils weighted by their %. Also the map unit key, Wind Erodibility Group (4L as 41), I, C and E. Cells on buildings, roads and water (§ above), or mostly on them in the weighted rule, are marked in a cover column and have no soil values.
- *Weather:* each grid cell gives the means of its weather cell (gridMET and PRISM 1/24°, NASA POWER 0.5° by 0.625°, or your own daily file, one weather for the whole area at its centre, read as on the data sheet with its gaps filled from gridMET's cell there) over the dates you choose (dates outside the source's record are left out, and the status and README say so): rain and ET₀ per year or calendar month from the days with data, the rest as means. A number points to its row in `weather/cells.csv`, with a daily table for each weather cell, because a daily series for every grid cell would be far too large.
- *Files:* a GeoTIFF (Float32, Deflate, one band per column with its name, NoData −9999, square cells only; a turned grid carries its turn in a transformation matrix, which some software ignores), a shapefile, GeoJSON and CSV of the cells, `bands.csv`, `soil_units.csv` and `crs.txt`. Checked with rasterio and geopandas: every pixel's centre is its lattice cell's centre, also when turned, and polygons are valid and tile without overlap, with outer rings clockwise in the shapefile and counter-clockwise in GeoJSON (RFC 7946), holes the other way, whatever the winding of your own grid. Hexagons can only be polygons. A shapefile holds 240 columns; with more, it is not offered, and Build is refused while no ticked file can be made. Limits: 50 million cells in the area's box, 1.5 billion values in a GeoTIFF, 500,000 polygons in a shapefile, 100,000 in GeoJSON, 1 million CSV rows.
- *Steps, progress and Download:* the sheet lists the steps (draw or upload, make a grid, choose, Build and preview, choose the columns and Download). Before Build it says what Build will read over the internet and roughly how long it takes, worked out from the choices alone so it costs nothing: the soil layers of the map units not read yet (3 Soil Data Access queries per 100 map units, two batches at a time), the weather cells the area's box touches (the most it can need; those read before are kept for the page) and their requests by source, from about 1 s a gridMET request and 0.2 s more for each year of days (2026-11-11: 4 cells over 36 years took 16 s), 6 at a time, and 8 s for 10 years of PRISM through ACIS, 4 at a time; above 30 s or 2 million cells it asks for patience. Build and Download show a bar: the step (soil, cells, weather, columns; tables, GeoTIFF, cells, zip), what it is doing with counts (map units, cells weighed, weather cells, gridMET requests, GeoTIFF strips, shapefile cells, the zip), the percent where it can be counted and the time so far, and a Cancel button. Cancel stops the work at its next check between batches, and a wait on the network ends at once; the sheet stays as it was, and soil and weather read so far are kept for the next Build. A change to the grid, soil or weather choices while it builds stops it too, as its cells would no longer match. After Build a Download section lists what the zip will hold, with rough sizes (a GeoTIFF's before Deflate, as the most it can be), and after Download the exact sizes. The files ticked and the coordinates of the shapefile and CSV are read at Download, so they can change without building again; each is checked then against the cells built.
- *Speed:* gridMET is read for all the weather cells at once: one OPeNDAP request a variable for the box of cells, or each row's span when the box would be mostly empty, split by years so no answer passes 2 million values, so 9 requests in place of 9 for each cell (18 before each variable's packing, its `.das`, was kept for the page). At most 6 requests are in flight to THREDDS from the page. Checked against a request for each cell: all 116,896 values of 4 cells over 10 years (one of them 100 km away, so read by row spans) were the same. 9 weather cells over 10 years took 4.4 s, against 28 s a cell at a time, 3 at a time, with the `.das` already kept (2026-11-11, Lubbock); a 100 km² square at 30 m (110,465 cells, 22 map units, 9 gridMET cells, 10 years) built in 6 s. PRISM reads ACIS once a cell, 4 cells at a time (3 before), with its solar radiation, humidity and wind from the gridMET box; NASA POWER stays at 3 at a time. A weather cell that does not answer is tried once more and then left blank: its weather columns are NoData, it has no daily table, and the status and README name it with the reason, so the rest still build; when not one weather cell answers, Build stops and says why, as a grid with all its weather blank is no use. Soil layers on the chosen bottoms are worked out once for each soil component, not once for each layer bottom. The estimate waits until typing pauses (300 ms). The preview works out a value's colour once, draws at screen resolution above 250,000 cells and your own grid's cells as their boxes above 50,000, with a note. GeoTIFF strips are deflated four at a time while the next is filled (5–7% quicker in Chromium, where the compression itself takes the time).
- *Data for models:* the data sheet's model files (§7: DSSAT, SWAT+, EPIC, APEX, APSIM, AquaCrop, CENTURY, DayCent, plain CSV) for the whole area, from the same code (`js/datasheet.js` exports its choices, the sources' records, the read of a weather file and the 10-year climate check; `M.compSoil` makes a component's profile as for a point), written once for each soil and weather cell rather than for every grid cell. Choices: the models; soil and weather, soil only or weather only; each model's usual layers, the horizons as they are, or custom depths. *Soil:* the grid's Soil Data Access query takes the point's component and horizon fields (slope, hydrologic group, drainage class, dry albedo, taxonomy, texture, the map unit's slope), so each map unit gives a full profile: under "Within a map unit" its main soil, the largest component whose layers have water retention and bulk density, or all such components as one profile, each layer the mean of theirs weighted by their % (organic matter, carbon, CaCO₃ and N also by bulk density; the components lie side by side, so Ksat too is an arithmetic mean), on the union of their horizon bottoms or your depths, a component that ends above a layer leaving it to the others; slope, albedo and drainage rate weighted the same way, the hydrologic group, drainage class and family those of the largest. Miscellaneous areas (urban land, rock outcrop, pits) are left out, even over buried horizons; a map unit with nothing else has no model soil, and `models/soils.csv` says why. *Weather:* the sheet's weather (3 · Weather), each weather cell read at the elevation of the middle of the grid's cells in it (the Copernicus 90 m DEM through Open-Meteo, all cells in one request, kept for the area so a later Build reads the same weather; failing that, the survey's elevation of the main soils there), as the point sheet reads at the point's elevation (gridMET's humidity and dew point depend on it). When the dates lack what TAV and AMP, the generator statistics or CENTURY's and DayCent's climate lines need, 10 complete years of the source are read for each weather cell, gridMET for all of them at once, and kept for the page, so a later Build reads them only for new weather cells. *Runs:* each soil with each weather cell that some grid cell has (only the soils, or only the weather cells, when the files hold only those). CENTURY's and DayCent's site files hold the soil with its weather cell's climate, so they are written for each run, naming the weather cell's file (5,000 site files and 40,000 sets of files at most, counted from the soils and cells before the weather is read, so too many is said at once). *Layout:* `models/<model>/weather/` and `soils/` with the writers' own names (weather by the weather cell's coordinates, soils S + the map unit key); DSSAT's profiles in one `soils/WG.SOL` and a station code for each weather cell (made unique); SWAT+ as one TxtInOut set (a station for each weather cell in pcp.cli and the other lists, weather-sta.cli and weather-wgn.cli, a soil for each map unit in soils.sol and the QSWAT+ table; SWAT2012 stations w001pcp …); files with fixed names (plain CSV, DayCent's soils.in and sitepar.in) in a folder for each weather cell, soil or run. Each model's README.txt says how its folder is laid out, then gives the data sheet's README for each part; NOT_WRITTEN.txt lists any part that could not be made, with the reason, and the others are complete. *Links:* number columns soil_id (the map unit key), wx_id (the weather cell, as wx_cell) and run_id; in the shapefile, GeoJSON and CSV also each model's names as text (DSSAT_SOIL is the profile's ID_SOIL, DSSAT_WX the WSTA, SWAT_WX the station, the others the file); `models/runs.csv` lists each run with its grid cells, area and each model's files, `soils.csv` and `weather.csv` the soils and weather cells. In the weighted rule a cell's soil_id and run are those of the map unit covering most of it, as its mukey. Checked (2026-11-11, Lubbock): every cell's run_id matched its soil_id and wx_id in runs.csv, and the runs' cell counts matched the cells, by centre and weighted, square and hexagons; a 100 km² square at 30 m (113,423 cells, 25 soils, 12 gridMET cells, 119 runs) wrote all nine models' files, 32.6 MB in 1,223 files, in about 2 s after its weather was read, and packed to 9.3 MB in 4.5 s.
- *Download choices:* the columns of the files are chosen after Build, one by one or by preset: erosion (cover, the map unit, WEG, I, C, E, the top layer's texture, organic matter, CaCO₃ and fragments, the weather cell, rain, ET₀ and wind), soil properties, weather, model links, all (the default). The model link names are text, so they go in the shapefile, GeoJSON and CSV only; a GeoTIFF needs a number column. The model folders are ticked there too; `models/README.txt` and the index always come with them. The Download list gives each model folder's files and size.

**Map-click check:** before a clicked point (or a dragged pin) is analysed, the same three layers are read for a ~180 m box around it. If the point falls inside a building or water body, or within a road's half-width, it is refused with the reason. Typed coordinates, GPS and place search are not checked. If the services don't answer within 3.5 s the point is accepted.

## 6. Recommendations (Practices tab)

Texas has its own engine (`plan` in `js/practices.js`), written from `docs/RESEARCH.md`: Texas NRCS standards, AgriLife, TxDOT, University Lands and others. Every other state and DC has a profile, `data/practices/XX.json`, loaded when its Practices tab first opens, read by a national engine (`planState`) with the same land uses and questions; the state's crops and shrubs fill the questions, and the site's MLRA (SSURGO) picks the state's region.

**How the profiles were made.** `tools/fetch_efotg.py` indexed each state's NRCS Field Office Technical Guide (eFOTG) and downloaded, with their text and tables, the current documents of the wind-erosion practices (314, 327, 328, 329, 340, 342, 345, 380, 382, 386, 484, 511, 512, 528, 550, 585, 588, 589 cross wind trap strips, 603, 609, 612, 650: standards, implementation requirements, specifications, guidance) and the state's references on wind erosion, establishment, species, seeding, trees and grazing. Maryland and DC have no eFOTG folders; their short profiles rest on a few official web pages. Kansas was researched in depth (its documents and K-State, the Kansas Forest Service and other public sources) and every statement checked against its source by a second reader, who corrected or removed 20. The other states were drafted from their eFOTG text by one quick reader each (full detail for the 17 wind-erosion states of the Plains and the West, short for the humid East), then a second quick pass filled missing cover-crop, seeding-date, native-species and windbreak lists from the same documents and, for seven western states whose documents lacked them, from a few official extension or NRCS pages (California, Colorado, Nebraska and Utah gained items; South Dakota, Wyoming and Nevada none). All were checked by `tools/build_practices.py --check`: every number in a statement, a species row or a date window, and every species name, must appear in the text of a source it cites, or the item is dropped. That check catches wrong or invented numbers, not a wrong reading of a correct one, so these profiles are thinner and less vetted than Kansas's; each lists in `gaps` what was not found. Every statement carries its sources, and the state's current practice standards are added as sources.

**How the plan uses a profile.** The practices and their order follow the same logic as in Texas (the land use, the answers, the soil's WEG, the risk and the windy months from the wind history). In each practice the first state statements are bullets with their sources and the rest are under "More from <state>'s sources"; the state's facts are shown as their sources write them, in US customary units. The national parts are the same everywhere: the cover target by WEG, NebGuide's flat-cover reduction, the NAM residue table and the WEG notes.

**Texas key thresholds:**

- **Cover targets:** ≥ 60% surface cover on WEG 1–2, ≥ 40% on WEG 3–4L, ≥ 30% otherwise.
- **Flat-cover erosion reduction:** 10% → 35%, 30% → 70%, 50% → 85%, 70% → 93% (NebGuide G1537).
- **Residue table:** NAM Exhibit 502-1 E-table ratios (I 86, C 100, wide field), scaled to the site's E. This is approximate.
- **Cover crops:** 30 lb/ac rye or wheat on dryland; terminate 6–8 weeks before cotton (dryland) or 2–4 weeks (irrigated). Source: AgriLife Lamesa/Lubbock trials.
- **Barriers:** ≥ 1.5 ft tall, 40–50% porous, ≤ 10H apart (TX 603). Trap strips ≥ 15 ft wide (589C). Ridges ≤ 4× height apart (588). Windbreak protects 10H leeward and 2H windward (TX 380).
- **Grazing:** 25% harvest efficiency (≤ 33% in lesser prairie-chicken range). Drought checkpoints Apr 1, Jun 15, Jul 15, Aug 15, Nov 1 (TX 528).
- **Seeding:** Feb 1/15–May 15, or the monsoon in the Trans-Pecos. Drill rows ≤ 12 in, ¼–½ in deep. Defer grazing ≥ 1 growing season (TX 550). TxDOT 2024 Item 164 district mixes; University Lands Permian mix.
- **Shinnery and sand sagebrush:** tebuthiuron about 0.3 lb a.i./ac; treat ≤ 50% of the property per 2 years; 150-ft dune buffer; defer grazing 2 years (WAFWA).

## 7. Model files and downloads (data sheet)

The data sheet is the panel's **DOWNLOAD data for models** tab, marked with a down arrow, in every map theme (the download button in the panel's header opens it too). It writes files for one location and a chosen date range. Every layout below was checked against the model's manual, its official example files and the reading code of its source (DSSAT-CSM 4.8.6 `IPWTH`/`IPSOIL`; SWAT+ rev 62 `cli_*`/`soil_db_read`; WXPM3020 and the EPIC/APEX user manuals; ApsimX `ApsimTextFile`, `Weather` and `Soil.Check`; AquaCrop 7.3 `LoadClim`/`LoadProfile`). A test runs every writer on 20 years of Lubbock weather and a SSURGO profile under each layering choice and passes all those checks. None of the files has been run in the models here. A model or table that cannot be made is replaced by a `NOT_WRITTEN.txt` giving the reason, and the rest of the zip is kept.

**Preview.** The Preview button runs everything a download runs except the zip, and draws what the writers were given: weather (`js/preview.js`), by day, month or year, rain and ET₀ as sums of the days with data and the rest as means, values no station reports left out, with a table of days with data, missing days and ranges, and on request the gap-free copy of §7 *Gaps* over it; the soil layers of each ticked model's file (the functions its writer uses), as bars or as a profile with depth to scale; the hourly wind series by day, month or year and the monthly and annual summaries; and the files, with sizes and first lines, the notes and anything that will not be written. The weather and climate reads are kept, so Download after Preview reads nothing twice. A notice appears when a choice changes after the preview.

**Tabs.** The sheet's tabs make the weather files, the soil files, or both together in one zip: each model's writer runs with what the tab has, so its folder holds that kind of file only. Tables go with the tab they belong to. The Soil tab needs no dates, except that CENTURY's and DayCent's site files hold climate lines too, for which it reads 10 complete years of the source chosen under Weather files.

**Weather sources.** Dates outside the chosen source's record are left out, and the status line and README say so: gridMET runs from 1979 to its last day (about 2 days ago), PRISM from 1981 to about 3 days ago, NASA POWER from 1981 to about 8 days ago, and your file covers its own days unless gaps are filled from gridMET.
- gridMET (default): 9 variables from THREDDS for the chosen days. T = K − 273.15; Rs (MJ/m²/day) = W/m² × 0.0864; vapour pressure from specific humidity, ea = q p / (0.622 + 0.378 q), with p from elevation (FAO-56 Eq. 7); u₂ = 0.748 u₁₀; ET₀ is gridMET's grass reference.
- PRISM: rain, Tmax and Tmin from PRISM's 4 km daily grids through ACIS GridData, grid 21, which is documented and can be read from a web page. PRISM's own file service cannot (no CORS header, and a year would be about 2,555 files and 6.5 GB). ACIS's values match PRISM's raster: on 2023-09-15 at Lubbock's cell 56.2 against 56.17 mm and 25.93 against 25.93 °C, and whole years within rounding. PRISM has no wind, and ACIS no humidity or solar radiation, so those come from gridMET, or NASA POWER when gridMET does not answer, and the status line says so. ET₀ is FAO-56 Penman–Monteith from these (Hargreaves on days without radiation, dew point or wind). Lubbock 2023: 1,880 mm against gridMET's own 1,885, r 0.99.
  - *Dates.* PRISM's day D is the 24 hours ending at 1200 UTC on D, so it is mostly calendar day D−1. For calendar day D the app takes PRISM's date D+1, which lines it up with gridMET's wind and with stations: in 2023, Tmax against seven airports has r 0.993–0.998 that way and 0.88–0.96 as labelled. So the files differ by a day from PRISM's own. The offset is measured, not documented by gridMET.
  - *Limits.* Contiguous US. The last six months are provisional and can change up to eight times. Each README gives the credit PRISM's terms ask for: its name, address and the date read.
  - *Not used.* PRISM's Data Explorer back end also serves dew point, vapour pressure deficit and solar radiation and answers web pages, but it is undocumented, and nothing says other sites may call it.
- NASA POWER daily: ET₀ by FAO-56 Penman–Monteith (Hargreaves, Eq. 52, on days without radiation, dew point or wind).
- Your file: CSV, text or the first sheet of an Excel workbook (read as stored values: real dates, full precision, percentages as 0–100). The delimiter is the file's own (comma, semicolon or tab, with decimal commas where the comma is not the delimiter). Lines above the header row (NASA POWER, Open-Meteo, Campbell TOA5, PRISM, Daymet) are skipped. Units °C/°F, mm/in, MJ/W/kW/langley/kWh, m/s/mph/km/h/kn; wind at any height is moved to 10 and 2 m with the log law (z₀ = 0.03 m). Blank or text cells and codes of −99 or below are missing, and "T" (trace) rain is 0. Vapour pressure comes from the dew point, else from RHmax and RHmin (FAO-56 Eq. 17), else from mean RH (Eq. 19); the dew point follows from it. Gaps can be filled from gridMET for the same days (not ET₀, which would not match your weather). ET₀ is the file's own grass-reference column where it has one (et0, ETo, ETos, reference ET; never ETr or alfalfa-reference, pan evaporation or PET; in the rain unit), else FAO-56 Penman–Monteith from its columns after any filling, with solar radiation from the temperature range (Eq. 50) on days without it and Hargreaves (Eq. 52) on days without humidity or wind. The zip's README counts the days of each.
  - *Templates and units.* The sheet shows the layout and makes a template (`js/templates.js`): tick the columns you have, choose each one's unit, and download it as CSV or Excel. A column's name can state its unit, as the template's do (`tmax (F)`, `Rain_mm_Tot`, `SlrW_Avg`, `srad (kWh/m2/d)`, `WS_ms_Avg`), and a wind column its height (`wind at 3 m (mph)`, `WS10M`, `u2`); that is used for that column, and the choices in the sheet apply only to the others. Humidity of 1 or less on every day, or in a column named as a fraction (`rh (fraction)`, `(0-1)`), is read as a fraction. The sheet says what each column was read in, and notes a value that does not fit its unit (a typical Tmax above 55 °C, solar radiation above 45 MJ/m², wind above 25 m/s, a typical wet day above 100 mm).
  - *Columns* are found by name, tried on the name without units and notes in brackets. An exact name beats a pattern, and either beats a second choice: a column with a duration shorter than a day ("Rain_60min" gives way to "Rain"; a day's, as in "Precip 24 Hour Total", says nothing) or rain named an accumulation or "since" a time. Other measurements under the same words are never read: battery, panel, soil, surface, dew-point and wet-bulb temperatures; times of extremes; rain rates, hourly or weekly amounts and normals; net, longwave and clear-sky radiation; wind directions, gusts, maxima and runs. The data sheet and the README list which header each quantity was read from, so a wrong match can be seen.
  - *Dates* are chosen from the rows: a column of full dates first (YYYY-MM-DD, YYYYMMDD, also with a time run on as YYYYMMDDHHMM, or M/D/YYYY; D/M/YYYY when a first number in the column is above 12), else year, month and day, else year and day of the year, else any other column of full dates. "Day" holds the dates when "Time" holds clock times. A Campbell TOA5 daily record stamped at 00:00 holds the day before (2024-07-02 00:00:00 is 1 July). Impossible dates (30 February, month 13) are left out and noted.
  - *Cells.* The delimiter is the one that makes the same number of columns on most lines and gives a header row, as title lines can outnumber a short table's. A row of units under the header is read, as is a unit written in a column's cells (`40°F`, `0.1 in`); a cell in another unit than its column is a gap. Blank, text, −99 or below, and 999, 9999 and 6999 are missing, except where the column's unit can hold them (9999 kJ/m², 99.9 km/h, or tenths in GHCN's own CSV). GHCN's tenths and Daymet's daylight-mean radiation (with `dayl` in seconds) are recognised. Numbers in scientific notation and with lab flags are read; a cell that still does not read as a number is a gap, and the notes name the column, the count and the first such cell. A row of zeros in every column is a logger's placeholder, so a gap, as is a day whose rows are all such rows.
  - *Rows through the day.* When rows differ in their time on at least half the days, each day is summarised: the highest Tmax and maximum humidity, the lowest Tmin and minimum humidity, the mean of other humidity, dew point and wind, solar radiation added up for an energy and averaged for W/m². Each day's rows are put in time order first, also in a file that runs newest first. A logger stamps each reading at the end of its interval (01:00 to 00:00, or 00:00 to 00:00), so its midnight row belongs to the day before. This is recognised from the file's ends (it starts about one row after midnight, or ends on a midnight row alone on its day) and from its midnight rows: one that repeats the day before's last reading, above 0, while the next row changes says the stamps are at the interval's end; one that changes and goes on into the day, above 0, says they are at its start. Rain says so, and so does a daily solar or ET₀ value repeated on every row of the day before. Both ends are enough unless the midnight rows disagree; one end needs them to agree; with neither, a whole day must hold the value its midnight row repeats, and no midnight row may say the stamps are at the start. On rows through the day, a row of zeros next to a reading within 3 °C of it, before or after, is kept as a calm, dark, freezing hour, unless one of its zeros cannot be a reading: a humidity of 0, solar radiation of 0 next to daylight, or rain of 0 between two wet readings. That last is allowed only in a column of amounts (rain that falls back to 0 between wet rows, eases from one wet row to a smaller one, and falls twice within one day, which a total that resets once a day cannot), and only where the readings on both sides have a high and a low within half a degree of each other, as a real reading's two zeros do. Rain is added up unless it is one of these. A daily value repeated on each row: the same on every reading of each wet day, and on at least half of a usual day's rows on one of them at least; where dry hours are left blank it fills every row of its wet days, and where they are 0 a blank is a missing reading. A value over 300 mm held through each day, far above any change from day to day, is a season total instead. ET₀ and solar energy are read the same way, but a value on fewer than half a day's rows counts as the day's only where, on some day of the file, it is given within an hour of midnight, is more than a flat hourly amount could be (1.5 MJ/m², 0.8 mm), or would add up to more than a day can hold (50 MJ/m², 15 mm); then every day's value is read so. Otherwise hourly daylight amounts are added up. A running total that only rises within each day gives its largest value. It is recognised when it rises within the day on three days or more and most of its wet days are given to their end on their highest reading, or when it is given only when it changes. With fewer such days, it is recognised when its name says so (daily, today, since) or when it holds its value between rains to the end of each wet day. A day left blank before its first tip and then given on every row to its end counts as such a day. On rows a few minutes apart, a reading held three hours is a total in itself, not rain: so a day whose readings stop on a value held three hours also counts, where every wet day that stops early stops within the same hour of the clock (a total since 7 am left blank from its reset to the next tip), and so does one value held three hours from near midnight. The file's first or last day given on one row only (the day before's total) says nothing either way. A running total that resets at another hour (a logger's midnight row still holding yesterday's total, midnight UTC, 7 am) gives what each row rose by, and after a reset all it holds. It is recognised when its name says so, or when it holds its value between rains and falls at one hour of the day. The falls must come once a day, within two hours of the same clock time. At least one must come after a climb in steps, or each after a value held over a midnight; or they must all come within one hour of the same clock time, each after a value held three hours, where the rain before them began at different hours. A fall that comes after rows left blank is timed from the first blank row. A single fall counts after a climb in steps; on hourly rows, also the fall of a total held from the file's first row; on rows minutes apart, only after a value held three hours, or where the file's last reading is held three hours after it and the fall lands on blank rows or the total then climbs in two steps or more, or in one step to a value other than the one it fell from. A season total gives what it rose by each day. On rows through the day, what it rose by over a missing day is dropped: the day after one, like the file's first day, gives only what it rose by over its own rows. It is recognised when it is held over midnights and reset at most once a year (and not within a year of its start from 0 in the file), or when it never falls and is kept from one day to the next: kept so over two midnights or more, with readings on at least two rows of each day, whatever its name; kept over one midnight only, where its name does not say daily. A total that never falls and opens each day far above any rise is one too. A weighing gauge's total wavers. A fall of a tenth or less, or a fall to no less than half that is back to the reading before it within three readings (or is the file's last reading), is a dip, not a reset, and only a rise past the highest reading is rain. If the name says the total is a day's (since 7 AM, Rain Today), only a fall of a tenth or less is a dip; every larger fall is a reset. Daily rows of a season total, which never falls and holds its value over dry days (or stays well above any day's rise), are read the same way, and there the day after a missing one, like the file's first day, is a gap. A fall to less than half is a reset, and only once a year. A smaller fall is a dip: that day gets no rain, and only a rise past the reading before it counts. Each dip of more than one step, or one not back within three days, needs the value to hold from one day to the next more than five times for each; a dip of one step of the column's resolution that is back within three days (or is the last day) needs nothing, where the total rises past its highest somewhere and dips no more than once a week. Some shapes cannot be told apart from the data alone, so the reader takes the reading that is right on most real files: steady drizzle that ends on its highest hour reads as a running total; the same amount on every row for three hours or more on rows minutes apart, as one tip held to a reset; drizzle from a file's first hourly row, as a total carried in from before the file; a reset that lands one step below the total and is back on the next reading, as a gauge's dip; a total kept over a midnight that never falls, as a season total rather than a daily total whose first hour was wetter than the whole day before; pairs of equal daily values that each rise once, as daily amounts; daily values that drop two steps or more and come back, as daily amounts unless the value holds from one day to the next more than five times for each such drop; and a file that runs from 01:00 to a lone 00:00, as stamped at the hour's end unless its midnight rows say otherwise. The notes say which reading was used. A column's name helps where the rows cannot decide. On rows through the day, accumulated, cumulative or since a date (since Oct 1, since 1 Oct, since 10/1, since 2024-10-01) point to a season total, where the name has no day, daily, today, midnight, hour, hrly, minute, 24, other span (1d, P1D, 10m, 07-07, 0700-0700, since last obs) or time of day (since 7 AM, since 0700, since noon, since sunrise or sunset, 12Z, UTC, GMT, LST, local time). The name decides where the rows allow it: the total falls only a year apart. The rows win where it falls once a day at one hour on two days or more, or where every day given runs from its first reading on every row to the day's end and one is blank from the day's start or holds its value while other days are blank: that is a total that starts again each day or at some hour, whatever its name. They win too where a value given on rows in a row, holding (a fall of one or two steps of the column's resolution to more than half, or a 0, ends it; a reset does not), is then left blank, and after the blank rows the total goes on at under half the value held or more than two steps below it, or the file opens no more than ten times above its largest rise from one row to the next and ends on blank rows or opens on them: that is a total that resets at some hour and is left blank while it is 0, so each blank row counts as 0 and the first reading after blank rows adds all it holds (a season total is given on every row or only when it changes, and an outage of one shows neither; nor is a column whose dry hours are written 0 left blank while it is 0). Blank rows of less than a day between two equal readings above 0, where no reset seen in the file comes at that time of day and no higher reading follows within the hour, are an outage of the value held, not 0. In both tests a reading missing counts as given, not as a blank row: up to an hour of blank rows, or two rows where rows are more than half an hour apart, between two equal readings above 0, or up to an hour of blank rows between a reading and a higher one where the column also holds a value on rows in a row somewhere (across a reading missing too), in a column given on rows in a row for at least half its readings, where no reset seen in the file (a fall to under half or by more than two steps, or the blank rows that end it) comes at that time of day. Nothing is filled where the column holds one value only. Readings missing make the rows win by the first test on their own only where the rows rise or fall within a day, no reading after blank rows across a midnight is at or above the one before them (where that one is more than one step of the column's resolution), and none after blank rows is more than ten times the largest rise from one row to the next and more than 75 mm an hour could bring since the row before: otherwise it is a season total kept over an outage, or one whose first rows were lost. Where the rows cannot tell (no fall, one fall, given only when it changes, dry hours blank or 0), the name decides; a column with no reading above 0 is not read by its name. Read so by its name, a fall is a gauge's dip only if it is a step or two of the column's resolution, not to 0, and back within three readings to no more than a step or two above, or on the file's last three rows (or the last three before the blank rows that end it, the fall coming on the row after the reading before it); a day of blank rows is no missing day where the readings never repeat the one before (a total given only when it changes); and where 0s outnumber blank rows, the readings above 0 never repeat the one before and they open more than ten times above any rise, 0s that go back up past the reading before them are dry hours, not falls, and so are the file's first and last 0s (where some 0s went back up so, or where the readings above 0 make one run and 0s follow it); such dry hours are no falls when the rows decide either. A day's value on all its rows stays that day's rain under such a name too, unless a value holds over two days, or the values stay three times above any rise from day to day and never fall. Day, daily, today, since midnight or since a time of day point to a total that starts again each day or at some hour. On daily rows, accumulated, cumulative or since a date (not since a time of day, and without day, today, hour, or 24 outside the date) point to a season total once its value holds over two days. A column named for a season, a year or YTD is not read as rain. These rules were checked on 2024 hourly, 15-minute, 5-minute and daily rain at Lubbock, Tyler and Houston, written in each of these forms, oldest and newest first, in windows of 1 to 60 days. A few repeated days of a daily file keep each column's last value, and exact repeats count once.
  - *Limits.* A Tmax or Tmin outside −60…60 °C, solar radiation below 0.1 or above 50 MJ/m² a day, rain below 0 or above 1,900 mm a day, humidity of 0 or above 105 %, wind above 75 m/s and ET₀ above 50 mm a day are gaps, counted in the notes. When most of Tmax or Tmin are out of range the unit cannot be right, and the file is refused before anything is filled. So is a file with more than one station. YY/MM/DD dates are refused, since they cannot be told from D/M/YY in general.
  - *Notes* on how the file was read appear in the sheet and the README: the TOA5 shift, rows left out, repeated days, trace rain, and rain that only ever rises, which looks like a running total.

**Gaps.** DSSAT, APSIM and AquaCrop stop on a missing value, so their files get a gap-free copy. It runs from the first to the last day with both Tmax and Tmin; when that is shorter than the chosen dates, the status line and READMEs give the dates. Values no station reports are taken as missing first, in every model file and table: rain, radiation, humidity, vapour pressure or wind below 0, radiation above 50 MJ/m², a temperature outside −60…60 °C, and Tmax = Tmin = 0 (an empty row). Then Tmax, Tmin and wind are linear between neighbouring days and reach up to 3 days past the first or last value; a longer run of wind at either end takes that calendar month's mean over the days written (the overall mean if the month has no wind), counted separately in the README. A column is left out only when the weather has none of it. Solar radiation is 0.16 √(Tmax − Tmin) Ra (FAO-56 Eq. 50), rain 0, vapour pressure from the dew point or Tmin, and ET₀ FAO-56 from the other columns; a Tmax below Tmin is swapped. Each README counts the filled days. SWAT+ (−99), EPIC (999) and APEX (blank or 9999) get their missing-value codes and generate those days.

**TAV and AMP.** Monthly means of (Tmax + Tmin)/2 need 20 or more days each. DSSAT pools all years (TAV = mean of the 12 means, AMP = warmest minus coldest month, as CSM, WEATHER.CDE and the official headers use it). APSIM averages the same two numbers over complete calendar years (its `tav_amp`). When the chosen dates do not cover every month, the app reads the last 10 complete years from the same source (gridMET for your own file). If that read fails, TAV and AMP come from the months the dates have and are marked approximate in the file and its README.

**Weather-generator statistics.** EPIC/APEX `.WP1` follow WXPM3020 (wet day > 0 mm; they match its output for the official TX1136 example within 0.001). SWAT+ `weather-wgn.cli`: wet day ≥ 0.1 mm; the maximum half-hour rain is a third of each month's largest daily rain, averaged over the years in which the month is complete (no sub-daily data). Each group of statistics (temperature, rain, solar radiation, humidity, wind) comes from the chosen dates when every calendar month has 20 or more days of it, pooled over the years; else from the 10 complete years (read whenever the dates lack a month or a variable) when they have 20 or more; else from the chosen dates with 10 or more days in every month; else from the 10 years with 10 or more, the last two with a note naming the variable and months. Otherwise the model's files are not written (`NOT_WRITTEN.txt` says why), rather than written with zeros. Monthly rain totals and wet days are per whole month, from the days with data. P(W|D), P(W|W) and SWAT+'s wet_dry and wet_wet use only days whose day before has data; with no such day, the month's share of wet days.

**Wind height.** DSSAT and APSIM: 2 m (DSSAT WIND in km/day); SWAT+: 1.7 m, u₁₀ × 0.17^0.2; EPIC/APEX: 10 m. EPIC/APEX `.WND` take the monthly speed and 16-direction frequencies from the app's hourly wind; months it does not cover use the daily 10 m wind, with directions spread evenly.

**File names.** APSIM and AquaCrop files are named from the coordinates to 0.001° (about 100 m), e.g. `WG33578N101855W`, so nearby sites do not overwrite each other; EPIC and APEX want 8.3 names, so theirs are W + 7 letters and digits packed from the same coordinates. The DSSAT station code is a letter, a digit and two letters or digits made the same way.

**CENTURY 4.0 and 4.5.** Formats come from the model's own Fortran readers (`wthini.f`, `weathr.f`, `sitein.f`, `ckdata.f`), which win where the manual differs, and were checked with test copies of those READ statements; the 4.5 reader is known only from a third-party copy with NREL's change list behind it; Century 4.6 is not covered, as no format document for it was found. The files have not been run in the model. DayCent, the daily version, takes other files: see below. When the site file cannot be made and weather is in the download, the `.wth` is kept and `site_NOT_WRITTEN.txt` says why; values the cleaner set aside are counted in the README, as for the other models.
- *`NAME.wth`:* whole calendar years, three lines a year (prec, tmin, tmax), `format(a4,6x,12f7.0)`, 94 characters a line, no header, one line end after the last line (a blank line stops the run). Rain in cm a month, the valid days' total scaled to the whole month; tmin and tmax the mean of the days having both. A month with under 20 valid days, or outside the dates, is −99.99: the model takes TMN2M and TMX2M from the site file, and draws rain at random from PRECIP, PRCSTD and PRCSKW. Both temperatures are flagged together, as 4.5 stops on a month with tmin above tmax.
- *`NAME.100`:* one file for each version (240 lines each), because the model stops at the first label it does not expect: 4.5 adds ROCK, PRECRO and FRACRO, drops W1LIG, W2LIG and W3LIG, and gives EPNFS another meaning.
- *Climate lines:* FILE100's formulas: mean, population variance (dividing by n) and skew from it, on the monthly values; they reproduce NREL's own oakridge.100 in 11 of 12 months. From the chosen dates when each month has 10 years, as the manual asks, else from 10 complete years read for them, else with what there is and a note.
- *Soil:* SAND, SILT, CLAY, BULKD, PH and ROCK are one value each for the top 20 cm of mineral soil, as thickness-weighted means, since CENTURY has one texture per soil. Surface O horizons, and any without sand and clay, are left out and named, unless O horizons with data reach past 20 cm from the surface (counting O horizons without data above them, and a merged custom layer that starts with O under 20 cm of O); then the top 20 cm of organic material is used, and the README says the values are not mineral soil's. A gap between horizons is split between the two; a soil that ends above 20 cm gives means over what there is, and the README gives the depths used. Water layers follow ADEP in fix.100, assumed to be NREL's 15, 15, 15, 15, 30, 30, 30, 30, 0, 0 cm: AWILT and AFIEL thickness-weighted per layer with SWFLAG 0, and AFIEL at least AWILT + 0.01, as the water routine divides by the difference. NLAYER is the nearest boundary to the soil's thickness by the Workbook (to a restriction, else the greater of 60 cm and the base of the Bt), at most 8, because ADEP(9) is 0; a restriction in what was left out above does not count. Custom depths are averaged onto ADEP's layers, and the README says so. DRAIN from the drainage class by the Workbook's table.
- *Soil C:* b = Σ (OM/1.724) × BD × thickness × 100 g C/m² over the top 20 cm, split 0.03, 0.44, 0.53 among the active, slow and passive pools by the Workbook's table (IVAUTO 0). Without organic matter across those 20 cm, IVAUTO 1 starts from the Burke equation, unless it gives under 500 g C/m² (4.0 has no floor and goes negative on dry warm sites), when the pools start from 500.
- *Our choices, as no source gives them:* the thickness weighting; the 20-day threshold; SSURGO's water values rather than the model's own equations; surface active C of 10 and litter of 100 g/m², the values the model itself starts from; and the plant and nutrient lines from NREL's Colorado shortgrass sample (c3grs.100), which the README says to review.

**DayCent (December 2016).** For NREL's DailyDayCent of 21 December 2016, the download its page calls DayCent 4.7 (`js/daycent.js`). Formats come from that package's readers (`getwth.f`, `sitein.f`, `ckdata.f`, `initlyrs.c`, `setlyrs.c`, `initsite_tg.c`), which win where the manual of May 2018 differs, and each file was checked field by field with Python copies of those checks against NREL's Wooster run of the same package and its Oak Ridge, Harvard Forest and Duke runs. The files have not been run in the model. The schedule, `fix.100`, `outfiles.in` and the other event files are yours; the README gives the lines they need. When the site file cannot be made and weather is in the download, the `.wth` is kept and `site_NOT_WRITTEN.txt` says why.
- *`DNAME.wth`:* D + the same 7 letters and digits as CENTURY's name. One row a day, free format: day, month, year, day of the year, Tmax and Tmin (°C), rain (cm), and, when every row has them, solar radiation (langley/day = MJ/m² × 23.9006), relative humidity (%) and 2 m wind (mph = m/s × 2.23694). The model checks each row's month and day of the year against its own calendar, sets leap years from the year field, and goes back to the file's start at its end, so the file runs from 1 January of the first year to 31 December of the last: days outside the dates are −99.99, which the model fills with TMX2M and TMN2M and, for rain, 0 (so they are dry). A day missing Tmax or Tmin has both flagged; a Tmax below Tmin is swapped. With the extra columns `sitepar.in` gets `usexdrvrs 1`; without them (any day missing one, including padded days, since the model stops on a flagged driver) 7 columns and `usexdrvrs 0`, and the README asks for FWLOSS(4) = 0.8 in `fix.100`, as NREL does.
- *`NAME.100`:* 245 lines in DayCent's order: CENTURY 4.5's lines with the slow pool as a surface and a soil pool, SOM2CI(1,1), (1,2), (2,1), (2,2), and RCES2 for each; same labels as NREL's Oak Ridge, Harvard Forest and Duke files. Climate lines, soil C and the rest as for CENTURY, with the soil C in the soil slow pool and the surface one empty, SWFLAG and STORMF 0 as NREL's DayCent instructions ask, and BASEF 0.3 from NREL's Wooster run. DayCent replaces SAND, SILT, CLAY, BULKD, PH, AWILT and AFIEL with width-weighted means of `soils.in` (texture over the first CENTURY layer, 0–10 cm), so the site file holds those same means. PRECIP feeds N deposition and TMN2M, TMX2M flagged days; PRCSTD and PRCSKW are not used. TMN2M ≤ TMX2M is checked, as the model stops otherwise.
- *`soils.in`:* 13 columns a row (top, bottom, bulk density, field capacity, wilting point, evaporation coefficient, root fraction, sand, clay, organic matter, deltamin, Ksat in cm/s, pH). CENTURY layers follow ADEP 10, 20, 15, 15, 30, 30, 30, 30, 30, 30 cm (NREL's for DayCent), and the model stops unless each boundary down to NLAYER is a row's bottom, compared exactly, so depths are whole cm. NLAYER is the nearest boundary to the soil's depth (to its last horizon with water retention and bulk density), at most 9 (210 cm), as the model sets a larger NLAYER to 9; deeper soil is left out, and rows past a shallower soil's end repeat its deepest horizon. "Each model's usual layers" gives the rows of NREL's examples (0–2, 2–5, 5–10, 10–20, 20–30, 30–45 … 180–210 cm; the first three are its advice for trace gases); the horizons as they are and custom depths are split at 2 and 5 cm and the ADEP boundaries. The model takes at most 20 rows (it stops on a 21st), so more are merged, the thinnest pair inside one CENTURY layer first. Each row is the thickness-weighted mean of what it covers, organic matter by mass and Ksat harmonic (cm/s = mm/h ÷ 36,000), field capacity at least wilting point + 0.01; missing Ksat from Saxton & Rawls, organic matter 0.01 and pH 6.3, each counted in the README. Root fractions are those of NREL's Wooster and Oak Ridge files spread by depth (none below 105 cm or below a root-restrictive layer, unless it is at the top) and scaled to sum to 1; evaporation coefficients 0.8, 0.2, 0 and deltamin 0.09, 0.07, 0.05, 0.02 in the top rows are Wooster's, deltamin at most 0.8 × the wilting point so that the model's minimum water stays above 0.
- *`sitepar.in`:* NREL's file of the same package, keyword by keyword in its order, which the model's reader needs (older versions read it by position). Changed: `usexdrvrs` from the weather file, and tbotmn, tbotmx, the soil temperature range below the profile, for which no source gives a rule: the mean of the climate lines' monthly air temperatures ± half their yearly range × exp(−(z + 5)/d), z the profile's depth and d = √(2κ/ω) = 147 cm from the model's thermal diffusivity κ = 0.00215 cm²/s. Cloud cover is NREL's Wooster values, as placeholders; the README says so.
- *Our choices, as no source gives them:* as for CENTURY, plus the depth rule, the root fractions by depth, deltamin, the padding of partial years, and tbotmn and tbotmx.

**CO₂ (APSIM).** NOAA GML Mauna Loa annual means 1959–2025 (the 1959 value before that), then +2.5 ppm a year (AquaCrop's assumption). Without a co2 column APSIM Next Gen assumes 350 ppm. DSSAT and AquaCrop read their own Mauna Loa files.

**Elevation.** The download waits for the page's lookup, at most 25 s from when the sheet was opened, then takes NASA POWER's cell, then the survey's value, else it is unknown (DSSAT's .WTH −99; SWAT+, EPIC and APEX 0; gridMET's humidity at sea-level pressure). The top README has an Elevation line.

**A weather file used elsewhere.** It belongs to the place it was loaded at: at another location the sheet goes back to gridMET, with the file one click away and its distance stated in the sheet and README, and it is chosen again back at its own location.

**Your soil file** (CSV, text or Excel; the sheet makes a template in the same way, with examples or this location's survey layers as its rows) is read by name in the same way, with lab spellings (Upper/Lower depth, From/To, Sand total, SandPct, pH water, pHw, pH 1:1, pH (H2O), EC 1:1, ECe, Sat. paste EC, CaCO3 equivalent, CCE, CEC7, WP, FC, 15 bar, 1/3 bar, −1500 kPa, −33 kPa, and SSURGO's `sandtotal_r`, `wthirdbar_r` and the like). Not read: pH in CaCl₂, KCl or buffer, fine-sand fractions, base saturation, ECEC, fragments by weight, and `LL` unless a `DUL` column stands next to it (elsewhere it is the liquid limit). The unit in a header is converted (depth in mm, in, ft or m; g/kg; Ksat in cm/h, cm/d, mm/d, m/d, µm/s or in/h; EC in µS/cm or mS/m); water contents above 1 are read as percent, and a column at most 1 beside them only if as a fraction it would break wilting point < field capacity < saturation (a sand's WP 0.9, FC 6). By weight, `FC (% by weight)` is multiplied by bulk density. Sand and clay of at most 1 are fractions. `<0.1` is half the limit and `>200` the limit; a unit in the cells counts as the column's; a code such as 999 is missing unless the unit can hold it (EC 999 µS/cm), with a note. `% Saturation` alone is a salinity lab's and not used; beside WP or FC it is water by volume. A row with no depths (a Mean) is left out; a layer without texture or bottom is refused when others lie below it; layers that overlap mean more than one profile and are refused; values no soil has (BD outside 0.02–2.7, Ksat above 5,000 mm/h, EC above 300) are refused with the layer named. A blank organic matter stays blank where a file can do without (each README says what that model does), as does a blank CaCO₃ (DSSAT −99), while a given 0 stays 0; and estimates take at most 1 % and no more than the layer above. Organic carbon gives organic matter × 1.724 when there is no organic matter column. Sand and clay that do not fit (below 0, or above 100 % together) are refused with the layer named. The sheet names the columns it did not use.

**Soil.** The survey component's horizons (or your table) in the common structure of `js/models.js`. Horizons count from the first with water retention and bulk density to the first without (bedrock or a cemented pan ends the profile).
- *Layers:* "each model's usual" splits the horizons at DSSAT's standard depths (5, 15, 30, 45, 60, 90, 120, 150, 180, 210 cm) and APSoil's 150, 150, 300 … mm layers. "The horizons as they are" writes them unchanged. In any layering, a model that takes fewer layers merges the thinnest neighbours below the surface layer until they fit (DSSAT 20, EPIC 15, APEX and SWAT's user soil table 10, AquaCrop 5): by thickness, with organic matter, organic carbon, CaCO₃ and total N by mass, as below, and Ksat as a harmonic mean. "Custom depths" re-layers the profile: each layer value = Σ wᵢxᵢ / Σ wᵢ over the horizons it overlaps by wᵢ cm, and Ksat = Σ wᵢ / Σ (wᵢ/Kᵢ); organic matter, organic carbon, CaCO₃ and total N, fractions of the fine earth by mass, take wᵢ × BDᵢ (or wᵢ where no horizon has a bulk density), so a thin peat over mineral soil keeps its mass of carbon (exactly where the horizons' coarse fragments are alike, as BD is the fine earth's). APSoil's standard layers and the area grid's layers are made the same way. Depths past a profile that simply stops repeat the deepest horizon; depths past bedrock are cut there. The root growth factor is recomputed for each layer.
- *DSSAT `WG.SOL`:* soil ID WG + the 8-digit component key for an unedited survey soil on DSSAT's usual layers; any other layering, an edited surface or your own soil gets WG + 8 letters and digits made from the written soil table, so a profile with other layers or values never takes an earlier profile's ID (DSSAT uses the first profile in WG.SOL with a matching ID); columns as in §4, values right-aligned in 6-character slots of at most 5 characters (SSKS with fewer decimals when large); a second tier with CaCO₃ (g/kg) on the same rows.
- *APSIM `.apsimx`:* version 168 JSON; LL15, DUL, SAT from the survey with SAT ≤ 1 − BD/2.65 − 0.001; AirDry = 0.5 × LL15 in the top layer; KS = Ksat × 24 mm/day; Carbon = OM/1.724; crop KL 0.06 to 0.01 with depth, XF 1 (0 below a restrictive layer); SWCON 0.3; initial water at DUL; NO₃ 1 ppm, NH₄ 0.1 ppm; summer from 1 April in the northern hemisphere.
- *AquaCrop `.SOL`:* SAT, FC, WP in vol %; Ksat mm/day; CN by hydrologic group A 46, B 61, C 72, D 77 (Table 2.14d, 5% initial abstraction); REW = round(10 (FC − WP/2) × 0.04), 0–15; CRa and CRb from the soil class (Tables 2.24s-3, 2.24s-5); gravel 0 because the survey's water contents are for the whole soil; penetrability 100%, or 0 below a restrictive layer.
- *EPIC/APEX `.SOL`:* moist albedo = 0.625 × dry; field capacity and wilting point on a fine-earth basis, θ / (1 − fragment volume); blank pH as 7.
- *SWAT+ `soils.sol` and QSWAT+ table:* horizons without a survey bulk density are left out and named in a note, as in the official SWAT+ soil database (not custom layers, which are your depths and always have a weighted bulk density); depths in mm, carbon = OM/1.72, rock = % by weight > 2 mm, albedo = dry albedo, USLE K = the survey's rock-free K, or for your own layers K = f_csand f_cl-si f_orgc f_hisand (Williams 1995, SWAT theory 4:1.1); texture up to the 16 characters SWAT+ reads.

**The save dialog.** A chart, the wind rose or the map (§14) is saved through one dialog (`js/downloads.js`): a preview of the file, its size, resolution and format, and its texts edited in place.
- *Preview:* the file's own layout at the size chosen, drawn with only the pixels the screen needs. Fit scales it to the box (at most 2×), but never draws its text under 0.85 of its size: a tall file then fills the width and scrolls. Actual size is 1 CSS pixel per 1/96 in. It is drawn again 30 ms after the last change.
- *Sizes:* for a chart, as on screen, journal 1, 1.5 and 2 columns (3.5, 5.5 and 7.2 in wide), slide (10 in) or custom; for the map, §14. A size switched to another unit and back is the size typed: the boxes show it rounded, the dialog keeps it whole.
- *A chart's texts* (title, subtitle, legend labels, axis titles) are boxes laid over the preview where Chart.js drew them, in the same font, read from the chart after each drawing. What is typed goes into the chart's configuration (a legend label by its dataset, an axis title by its scale; the scales are copied, so the chart on the page keeps its own), and the preview is drawn again. The rose's texts are its SVG's own.
- *Leaving out:* a text emptied, or removed with its ×, is not drawn. It is listed under the preview to be put back, and Reset texts clears every change. Enter and Esc end the typing; Esc outside a text closes the dialog, and the focus goes back to the button that opened it.

**Figures and tables.** Charts are redrawn off screen at the chosen size, with the dialog's texts: pixels = inches × DPI, rounded to a whole pixel on each axis, fonts in points (1 pt = DPI/72 px). PNG gets a pHYs chunk, JPEG its JFIF density, and TIFF is written in the app (baseline RGB, PackBits, resolution tags in inches). CSV files carry a UTF-8 byte-order mark and the units in the headers. The annual wind summary marks partial years ("Complete year", and "Hours with data in the period" as a % of the year); the monthly summary writes "not ranked (part of a year)" for the months of a period that does not cover all 12 and "no data" for months without data, and heads its shares "% of the year" or "% of the months with data". The plain CSV has a README too, giving the ET₀ source and the layers. Each model folder's README gives the layers its soil file has (count, depths, bottoms).

**Units.** `js/units.js` holds one table of quantities, each with a base unit, choices, factors and decimals, plus metric, US and default presets. Display values convert from the base; classes stay defined in their source units (E and I in t/ac/yr, NRCS specifications in US units) and their edges are converted for display; model files ignore it and use each model's units.

## 8. Drought: map theme, live layers, tab and downloads

The map shows one theme at a time, chosen on the bar under the search box or in the layer sheet: **Wind erosion**, **Soil maps** (§11; with basins, §12, and soil moisture, §13) or **Drought**, in that order. A theme puts its overlay on the map (one at a time, the last one chosen) and gives the panel its tabs: Wind erosion has Overview, Wind, Soil and Practices; Drought has Drought; Soil maps has Overview, Soil, Basin and Soil moisture (§11–13, §4). DOWNLOAD data for models is in all of them. A link to another theme's tab (Wind erosion › Soil to Soil maps › Soil) switches the theme too. A tab row wider than the panel scrolls, and the open tab is scrolled clear of DOWNLOAD; on a phone the Soil maps theme's tabs read Overview, Maps, Profile. The theme is kept in the link (`&map=drought`, `soilmaps`), so a shared link opens on the same theme; a link naming a theme this app does not have opens on Wind erosion. Indices: SPEI over 30, 90, 180 days, 1, 2 and 5 years; SPI over 30, 90, 180 days, 1 and 2 years; the Palmer Drought Severity Index. The record can be cut to chosen years (from, to). For PDSI the last chart is the year's mean instead.
- *Which gridMET server:* gridMET Drought, like the gridMET daily weather behind the wind analysis's dryness and the model files (§3, §7), comes from the University of Idaho in two ways: THREDDS over OPeNDAP (a cell's whole series is a few kilobytes of text in one request) and the file server described below (the same series is megabytes of chunks). THREDDS is preferred while it answers; from 11 November 2026 its connections timed out for days at a time. The choice is made once a visit in `js/wind.js` and remembered in this browser (localStorage `fg.gmsrc`):
  - THREDDS failed less than 6 hours ago: the file server straight away, with no wait for THREDDS.
  - THREDDS answered less than a day ago: THREDDS straight away, with a check alongside the reads.
  - Otherwise a check comes first: rain's `.dds` on THREDDS (a few hundred bytes; it also gives gridMET's newest day), with 2.5 s to start answering. Meanwhile the Drought tab already reads the file server: that read is used if it ends first, and stopped if THREDDS answers first.
  - A read that fails on the chosen server moves to the other at once. Only THREDDS itself not answering is remembered as down: the check failing, no connection, a time limit, HTTP 5xx or 429. An answer a single read cannot use (a 4xx, a `.dds` with no time axis, text that does not parse) sends just that read to the file server.
  - While the file server is in use, THREDDS is asked again quietly 30 s into the visit (rain's `.dds`, 3 s to start answering): at most every 30 minutes while it is remembered down, and always when it failed earlier in this visit, so one slow answer does not cost 6 hours of downloads. The answer is remembered, and if THREDDS is back the rest of the visit uses it too.
- *What the tab says:* the This week card's note names its sources briefly: “Sources: U.S. Drought Monitor; gridMET Drought (University of Idaho, 4 km), via THREDDS; NOAA CPC outlooks.” It reads “via its file server” when the values came from there, and “via THREDDS and its file server” when each gave some. A value kept from an earlier visit (below) names the server it came from then. The full account is this section; the tab keeps to a few words.
- *Kept between visits:* each answer at a cell (dates and values, per index; the whole series and This week's last year apart) is kept for 6 hours in the app's Cache Storage box (`fg-keep-v1`, key `…/{index}.nc?fg-cell=j,i`, `,latest` added for This week). gridMET Drought gains a step only every 5 days, so going back to the same cell within 6 hours asks no server for it. For the file server, the layout `js/h5range.js` works out from a file's metadata (the superblock's sizes, the datasets read, the chunk-index nodes) is kept in the same box with the file's Last-Modified (key `…/{index}.nc?fg-h5-layout`); gridMET's yearly weather files keep theirs too. A later visit then asks for the chunks at once, skipping 4 to 6 metadata reads made one after another. Every byte range must still carry that Last-Modified, so a file rewritten since is caught at its first request, read again from its start, and its new layout kept. THREDDS's `.dds` and `.das` for an index are kept in the page for an hour.
- *Speed* (12 November 2026, THREDDS down, cells in Texas, timed from opening the tab): before these changes, This week's values took 23–28 s and the whole series 37–42 s, whether the visit was the first or not. Now a first visit shows This week's values in 2–4 s and the series in 6–8 s; with the files' layout kept (a later visit after the 6 hours), under 1.5 s and about 3 s; going back to the same cell within 6 hours, under 0.1 s.
- *The file server:* the same NetCDF-4 files, read in place on the University of Idaho's file server (`https://www.northwestknowledge.net/metdata/data/{spei90d,spi30d,…,pdsi}.nc`, 6–7.5 GB each; variables `spei`, `spi`, `pdsi`) by HTTP byte ranges with `js/h5range.js`, a small HDF5 reader: the superblock, the object headers and the variable's attributes (scale_factor, add_offset, _FillValue, kept in a fractal heap as there are more than 8), then the chunk index (a v1 B-tree), and only the chunks that hold the cell. Each chunk is 311 five-day steps of a 49 × 116 block, deflated: about 0.6 MB for SPI and SPEI, 1.9 MB for PDSI. A whole series is all of them (11 or 12 since 1980: 6–7 MB, PDSI about 20 MB, about 25 requests); this week's values need only the last (about 1 MB, PDSI 2.4 MB, 5 requests), or the one before when the last holds no value yet. Metadata comes in 64 KB blocks, chunk-index nodes and chunks as exact ranges. At most 6 requests go to the server at once, and the rest wait by priority: This week's values first, then a whole series, then reads in the background such as the wind analysis's yearly weather files on the same server. In each file the newest chunk is asked for first, since This week and the series share it, and the dates are read together with the values. The server rewrites the files every few days, so every answer must carry the first one's Last-Modified; a read that meets a new version is dropped and read again once. Values are the same as from THREDDS: raw × scale_factor + add_offset, the fill value missing, dates as days since 1900-01-01.
- *Classes:* the Drought Monitor's ranges for SPI and SPEI (Drought Classification, droughtmonitor.unl.edu): D0 −0.5 to −0.79, D1 −0.8 to −1.29, D2 −1.3 to −1.59, D3 −1.6 to −1.99, D4 −2.0 or less. That page gives no PDSI ranges, so PDSI is drawn as values only (below 0 drier). Colours are those of the Drought Monitor's maps.
- *Not used:* the Drought Monitor's statistics service (`usdmdataservices.unl.edu`), which gives a county's weekly record since 2000, sends no CORS header, so a web page cannot read it; its weekly GeoJSON is 16–17 MB a week, and NDMC's WFS and WCS are not enabled.

The USGS interpolates by inverse distance and colours 10 classes; sampling the WMS at the sites showed the classes are the national deciles of the sites (censored values at the detection limit, classes closed above). Values are total, not plant-available. Each site's name (Site 1–3) shows it as a point on the map.
- *SoilGrids:* a model; built-up land and water have no value.
- *KSSL:* the 8 nearest pedons' surface layer (`hzn_top = 0`) from `lab_combine_nasis_ncss`, `lab_layer` and `lab_chemical_properties`: NH₄OAc pH 7 K, Ca, Mg and Na; Bray-1, Olsen and Mehlich-3 P; total N and S. Sparse, often old, and Ca is inflated in calcareous soils. Each pedon's name shows it as a point on the map.
  - The marker comes with a dashed line from the location and a label with its id, year and distance, with both in view; on a phone the results drop to their bar.
  - One marker at a time. It goes when the location or the theme changes.
  - Station names on the Soil moisture tab work the same way.
- *Downloads:* every table of the Soil tab as CSV, each from its card and all of them from *Download all the tables*, the tab's last card. The Basin and Soil moisture tabs each end with a download-all card for their own tables.
- *What data is missing:* no map covers the US for soil-test (plant-available) N, P, K, Ca, Mg or S, or DTPA micronutrients; boron and phosphorus survey data is also limited for the US.
- *Basins and soil moisture:* §12 and §13 add their layers and the Basin and Soil moisture tabs to this theme.

## 12. Basins: runoff and soil loss (Soil maps theme, Basin tab)

Code: `js/basins.js`. Pre-built lookups come from `tools/build_basins.py`. It adds two map layers to the Soil maps theme and three cards to the theme's Basin tab, all for the 12-digit hydrologic unit (HU12) that holds the point.

**Map layers.**

| Layer | Source | How the app reads it | Shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Watershed boundaries | USGS and USDA-NRCS Watershed Boundary Dataset (public domain) | The National Map `wbd` MapServer `export` as 512 px tiles. dynamicLayers carry the app's own outlines, so the legend matches the map. Zooms 6–8 are asked at a lower dpi so the server draws HU8s there. | HU8 at zoom 6–9, HU10 at 10, HU12 (with its code) from 11 |
| Streams | USGS NHDPlus High Resolution (1:24,000 NHD with value-added attributes, public domain) | `NHDPlus_HR` MapServer layer 3 `export` tiles, classed by Strahler order (1–2, 3–4, 5–6, 7+) | from zoom 12 |

While the theme is on and the point is unchanged, the map also shows:
- the HU12's outline;
- its catchments, coloured by mean annual runoff (< 10, 10–25, 25–50, 50–100, 100–200, 200–400, 400–800, ≥ 800 mm/yr), with sinks grey and dashed;
- the point's catchment (red) and the catchments upstream of it (orange);
- the flowlines;
- the NLDI upstream basin (dashed), when asked for.

**The watershed.**
- The HU12 is found by a point query on WBD layer 6, its polygon simplified to about 10 m.
- Inside it, the NHDPlus HR flowlines (layer 3) and sinks (layer 1) are asked by the HU12's polygon. Their catchments are then asked by id from layer 10, 500 ids a POST, in pages of 2,000 and at most 20 pages. No spatial query is ever made on the catchment layer, as it takes 40–100 s or times out.
- A catchment counts as inside the HU12 when the centroid of its largest ring is.
- Upstream catchments are traced through the flowlines' `hydroseq`, `dnhydroseq` and `dnminorhyd`, inside this HU12 only. The card says when the stream also gathers water from upstream HU12s (`totdasqkm`).
- On request, USGS NLDI draws the whole upstream basin of the point's NHDPlus V2 catchment (`basin?simplified=true&splitCatchment=false`). It is not split at the point; its area is computed on the Albers plane.

**Mean annual runoff (model).**
- Each catchment's runoff is from NHDPlus HR's flow estimates, EROM step A (the McCabe-Wolock monthly water-balance model, 1971–2000 climate): runoff (mm/yr) = 893.6 × qincrama (cfs) ÷ area (km²). One cfs for a year is 893,600 m³.
- The HU12's runoff is 893.6 × Σ qincrama ÷ Σ area over its catchments that drain to the stream network. Catchments that drain to sinks (playas, stock ponds) have no EROM runoff and are reported apart.
- The card also gives the point's reach mean annual flow (`qama`, cfs and m³/s) and drainage area.
- It is a model, not a measurement; other models (EPA StreamCat on NHDPlus V2) can differ by a factor of two.

**Design-storm runoff (SCS curve number, NRCS TR-55 1986).**
- Storm: the 2-, 10- or 100-year 24-hour depth.
  - It comes from NOAA Atlas 14 (partial-duration series): the point's 0.05° cell (the mean of its 6 × 6 30″ cells), or the nearest of its 8 neighbours that has all three depths.
  - The volumes: 1 and 6 (AZ, CA, NM, NV, UT; one grid), 2 (DC, DE, IL, IN, KY, MD, NC, NJ, OH, PA, SC, TN, VA, WV), 8 (CO, IA, KS, MI, MN, MO, ND, NE, OK, SD, WI), 9 (AL, AR, FL, GA, LA, MS), 10 (CT, MA, ME, NH, NY, RI, VT), 11 (TX) and 12 (ID, MT, WY).
  - A state's cells take its own volume; a neighbouring volume fills only the edge cells outside the state that its own grid leaves empty.
  - Oregon and Washington have no Atlas 14 volume yet (NOAA Atlas 2 still applies there), so the depth is typed. A depth can be typed anywhere, with a link to NOAA's PFDS at the point (PFDS sends no CORS header).
- **Here, at the point** (the card's first part):
  - Land cover is the class of the point's own 30 m NLCD 2021 cell: the one the app read at the click, or a 3 × 3-cell read from the MRLC WCS.
  - The soil group is the dominant soil's hydrologic group, from the survey read for the wind erosion; a dual group is taken as undrained.
  - Its TR-55 curve number and the storm's Q, with S and Ia, are shown.
  - Open water and ice have no curve number.
- **The whole HU12** (one line):
  - Soil groups: SSURGO hydrologic groups, area-weighted inside the HU12. Soil Data Access intersects the HU12 with the map-unit polygons on its own server, and each component is weighted by its map unit's area × comppct. Dual groups (A/D, B/D, C/D) are taken as undrained (D). Area with no group is left out and its share stated.
  - Land cover: NLCD 2021, counted in 30 m cells inside the HU12 from the MRLC WCS (`js/landcover.js`). Open water and ice are left out.
  - Q is computed for each land cover and soil group, then averaged by their shares, because Q is not linear in CN. The line gives the area-weighted CN, Q, Q as a % of the rain, and the volume.
  - Each class's share and curve numbers are in `basin_landcover` (CSV).
- **The Land cover card (Soil maps › Soil).** Each NLCD class's share, in % and km²:
  - of the state on the map (Texas for now), pre-built (`data/states/landcover.json`); or
  - of your boundary when the area tool has one, counted live (if MRLC does not answer, the states' shares, with a note).

  Each class has its official colour, the TR-55 cover it is taken as and its CN for groups A–D. Classes under 0.05% are left out of the table, not of its CSV (`landcover`, also in the tab's file of all its tables). The card is drawn again when the boundary or the states change. The Basin card links to it.
- **The land cover key.** With Land cover (NLCD) on, from any theme or the Soil tab's tile, the map legend gives a compact key: the 16 classes' colours in two columns, and a link that opens Soil maps › Soil at the Land cover card (with the land cover kept as the map there). The key goes under the theme's legend, or alone with its own hide button. A saved map image keeps the key and leaves the link out. On a wide screen the legend keeps below the theme bar and scrolls inside, with its title and hide button held at its top.
- Curve numbers come from TR-55 Table 2-2, at average wetness (AMC II):

| NLCD class | Treated as | CN for A / B / C / D |
|---|---|---|
| 21 | open space, fair | 49/69/79/84 |
| 22 | residential, 1/4 ac lots (38% impervious) | 61/75/83/87 |
| 23 | residential, 1/8 ac lots (65%) | 77/85/90/92 |
| 24 | commercial (85%) | 89/92/94/95 |
| 31 | fallow, bare soil | 77/86/91/94 |
| 41–43 | woods, fair | 36/60/73/79 |
| 52 | brush, fair | 35/56/70/77 |
| 71 | pasture or range, fair | 49/69/79/84 |
| 81 | pasture, good | 39/61/74/80 |

- Runoff: Q = (P − Ia)² ÷ (P − Ia + S) for P > Ia, else 0, with S = 25400/CN − 254 mm (1000/CN − 10 in) and Ia = 0.2 S.
- HU12 CN = Σc ac Σg hg CNc,g; HU12 Q = Σc ac Σg hg Q(P, CNc,g).
- Volume V = Q × the HU12's land area (m³; ÷ 1233.48 = acre-feet).
- Land cover and soils are taken as independent inside the HU12; there is no cell-by-cell overlay.
- Limits: an event method for one storm on average wetness, not a forecast or a yearly total. It is crude for cracking clays (the Blackland Prairie's Vertisols) and for frozen or very wet ground.

**Curve number: the variables.**

| Symbol | Name | Meaning | Units | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P | Rainfall depth | The storm's rain in 24 hours. The 2-, 10- and 100-year storms have a 50%, 10% and 1% chance of being reached or passed in any year. | mm or in | NOAA Atlas 14 at the point's 0.05° cell (partial-duration series), or typed |
| CN | Curve number | 30 to 100: how readily the land sheds rain, from its cover and soil group, for average wetness before the storm. Higher means more runoff. | none | TR-55 Table 2-2 for the NLCD 2021 class and the soil group |
| AMC II | Antecedent moisture condition II | Average soil wetness before the storm, which Table 2-2 assumes; drier or wetter ground sheds less or more. | — | TR-55 |

| S | Potential retention | The most water the land can still take in once runoff has begun: S = 25400/CN − 254 mm (1000/CN − 10 in). | mm or in | from CN |

**Soil loss (RUSLE, USDA Agriculture Handbook 703).** A = R × K × LS × C × P, in short tons per acre per year (× 2.2417 = t/ha/yr).
- **R:**
  - USGS RF7100 (Wieczorek & LaMotte 2010, from the PRISM-based 1971–2000 R of Daly & Taylor): the mean R of the point's NHDPlus V2 catchment, whose COMID comes from USGS NLDI `comid/position`.
  - Units are hundreds ft·tonf·in/(ac·h·yr); × 17.02 = MJ·mm/(ha·h·yr).
  - It is pre-built per state and kept to 1%. In DC it is read from Maryland's and Virginia's files. R can be typed.
- **K:** the surface horizon's (hzdept_r = 0) Kw, or Kf where Kw is blank, from SSURGO. × 0.1317 = SI.
- **LS:** from each component's representative slope s (%) and USLE slope length λ (m).
  - S follows McCool et al. (1987): S = 10.8 sin θ + 0.03 for s < 9%, and 16.8 sin θ − 0.5 for s ≥ 9%, with θ = atan(s/100).
  - L = (λ / 22.13)^m, with m = 0.2 (s < 1%), 0.3 (1–3.5%), 0.4 (3.5–5%) or 0.5 (≥ 5%).
- **K × LS** is formed for each component and then averaged with the component weights (map-unit area × comppct). The card states how much of the HU12's area each factor covers.
- **C:** the NLCD classes' mid-literature values, area-weighted over the land: 21 0.005; 22 0.003; 23 0.001; 24 0; 31 0.45; 41–43 0.003; 52 0.025; 71 0.03; 81 0.035; 90 and 95 0.003. Cropland (82) takes the user's C, 0.24 by default.
  - *The C box* takes a value above 0 and at most 1: C = soil lost under the crop and its tillage ÷ soil lost from tilled bare fallow, so 1 = bare tilled soil and 0.24 = 76% less. Next to it are rough typical ranges (conventional-till row crops about 0.2–0.5, conservation or mulch tillage about 0.1–0.25, no-till about 0.02–0.15, small grain about 0.05–0.2, hay or meadow under about 0.02; a field's own C depends on its rotation, yields and residue, and NRCS RUSLE2 gives it). Under the box: "C 0.24: 76% less than bare tilled soil".
  - A value outside 0–1 (0, 1.5, 100) is refused: the box is marked invalid, a note says why, and the last valid C is kept.
- **P: the support practice, chosen in a list and applied to cropland (NLCD 82) only; other land takes P = 1.** It is worked out for each soil component from its slope s and USLE slope length λ (× 3.2808 = ft):
  - *None* (up and down the slope): P = 1.
  - *Contour farming* (AH 537, Table 13): Pc by slope class, with the longest slope it holds for:

    | Slope | Pc | Longest slope |
    |---|---|---|
    | under 2.5% (1–2%) | 0.60 | 400 ft |
    | 2.5–5.5% (3–5%) | 0.50 | 300 ft |
    | 5.5–8.5% (6–8%) | 0.50 | 200 ft |
    | 8.5–12.5% (9–12%) | 0.60 | 120 ft |
    | 12.5–16.5% (13–16%) | 0.70 | 80 ft |
    | 16.5–20.5% (17–20%) | 0.80 | 60 ft |
    | 20.5–25.5% (21–25%) | 0.90 | 50 ft |
    | over 25.5% | 1.0 | — |

    Where λ is longer than the limit, rows overtop in big storms and contouring is taken to fail: P = 1. Slopes under 1% take the 1–2% class.
  - *Contour strip cropping* (AH 537, Table 14; a 4-year rotation of row crop, small grain with meadow seeding and 2 years of meadow): Ps = Pc / 2 (0.25–0.45), with twice the length limits (800, 600, 400, 240, 160, 120, 100 ft). P = 1 beyond the limit or above 25.5%. Alternate strips of row crop and winter grain would take the contour values.
  - *Terraces, contour-farmed, closed outlets:* P = Pc × Pt.
    - Pc is the contouring factor for the slope, its limit checked against the widest terrace spacing of the class chosen (110, 140, 180, 225, 300 ft): the terraces leave that slope length.
    - Pt is the terrace sediment-delivery subfactor of Foster & Highfill (1983), as RUSLE uses it (AH 703, chapter 6): under 110 ft 0.5, 110–140 ft 0.6, 140–180 ft 0.7, 180–225 ft 0.8, 225–300 ft 0.9, over 300 ft 1.0.
    - Pt credits the soil that settles in the terrace channels.
    - The app keeps the survey's slope length rather than cutting it to the spacing, so A with terraces is on the high side.
    - AH 537's own Table 15 for terraced fields goes by slope only; the spacing values are not from it.
  - *Your own P:* above 0 and at most 1 (P = soil lost with the practice ÷ soil lost farming up and down the slope: 1 = no saving, 0.85 = 15% less, 0.5 = half). A value outside that (0, 1.5, 100) is refused with a note, and the last valid P is kept.
  - The list shows each practice with its P range and its P over this HU12's soils (weighted by K × LS, as A takes it) and the share of soil loss it saves, e.g. "Contour farming · P 0.5–0.9 by slope · here P 0.55, 45% less soil loss".
  - RUSLE proper works P out from ridge height, storm erosivity and furrow grade; these are the classic table values.
- **A over the HU12:** A = R × [⟨K·LS⟩ × (C − a82 C82) + ⟨K·LS·P⟩ × a82 C82].
  - ⟨ ⟩ is the component-weighted mean; a82 is cropland's share of the land; C82 is cropland's C.
  - The P shown is ⟨K·LS·P⟩ ÷ ⟨K·LS⟩.
  - The cropland-only A is R × ⟨K·LS·P⟩ × C82.
- **T:** SSURGO's tfact, weighted the same way. A / T is shown.
- **Soil moved each year** = A × land area (km²) × 247.105 ac/km², in short tons (× 0.90718 = t).
- Limits:
  - Gross sheet and rill erosion on the slopes, a long-term yearly average. It does not cover gullies, stream banks or channels, or the sediment that leaves the HU12.
  - LS comes from survey slopes, not a DEM.
  - C from land-cover classes is coarse: crops, residue and tillage change it ten-fold.
  - RUSLE fits rangeland, urban land and wetlands poorly.

**RUSLE: the variables.**

**The tab.** Each card is labelled model, satellite or station.
- **Today here:** the models at 0–7 and 28–100 cm (ECMWF IFS's mean of today's hours, partly forecast; else ERA5-Land's latest day) and the nearest station's latest reading at its shallowest sensor. The Overview's two tiles use the same reads.

Daily is offered up to 3 years, weekly from 14 days, monthly from 60 days. Each step is the mean of its days, blank where more than a tenth of them have no value.
  - Average over: none, or the 5, 10, 20 or 30 whole years before the period starts (none before 1950).
  - Model depths: 0–7, 7–28, 28–100 and 100–255 cm.
  - Sources: ERA5-Land (model, since 1950), ECMWF IFS (model, last 92 days), SMAP L4 (satellite, since 31 March 2015) and Stations (the 3 nearest).
  - The choices are kept while the page is open, for any location.
- **Models by depth:**
  - All the chosen depths on one chart: ERA5-Land solid, ECMWF IFS dashed.
  - ERA5-Land (Copernicus C3S, ECMWF; Muñoz-Sabater et al. 2021; 0.1°): daily means of the local day, read by calendar year, a run of up to 40 years a request, and kept for the location.
  - ECMWF IFS (9 km): hourly values averaged by local day, the past 31 days, or 92 when the period reaches into them, plus today.
  - Both come through Open-Meteo (CC BY 4.0), with one retry.
  - The numbers table gives, per depth: today (IFS), ERA5-Land's latest in the period, the period mean, the average over the chosen years on the same calendar days, the difference (mean − average) and the range of daily means.
- **Year against year:**
  - ERA5-Land at one chosen depth over the period's calendar days (the window ending on its last day's date) in each chosen year. Steps are daily up to 120 days, else weekly. A period longer than a year compares the calendar months of each year.
  - Up to 8 years (added in a list, dropped by their chip, each keeping its colour), and the average over the chosen years.
- **SMAP L4 and the National Water Model:**
  - SMAP L4 daily averages of the 3-hourly analyses at the point's 9 km cell, top 0–5 cm and 0–100 cm, over the period's last 366 days at most.
  - Source: USDA NASS Crop-CASMA (George Mason University) WPS `GetProfileByDate`, products `SMAP-9KM-DAILY-TOP` and `-SUB`, the cell asked in EPSG:5070 metres, GET only. Crop-CASMA serves 200 days a request.
  - The National Water Model on request: the latest hourly land analysis, read in place by byte ranges (`js/h5range.js`, as before).
- **Nearest stations:**
  - The 3 nearest within 400 km, with distance and direction. Their names are links that show them on the map.
  - Each gives its latest hourly reading (when the period ends within 2 days of today) and daily means over the period's last 2 years at most, at the sensor depths chosen (checkboxes).
  - Each station has its own chart and table. AWDB and USCRN are read as before.
- **The values shown:** two tables to open.
  - Every line drawn over the period, by step, in `moisture_shown`.
  - The years compared, in `moisture_years`.
- **Download all the soil moisture tables:** the tab's last card puts every table in one file, `soil_moisture_tables.csv`.

**Tables (CSV):**
- `moisture_models`: source, kind, top and bottom (cm), date, value, note, for every day of the period;
- `moisture_years`: the years compared at one depth, by calendar day, week or month, with the average;
- `moisture_smap`: SMAP and the NWM;
- `moisture_stations`: station, network, id, distance, sensor depth, date or time, m³/m³ and % by volume, daily mean or latest reading;
- `moisture_shown`: every line on the charts by step (from, to).

The charts are inline SVG; hovering over a day lists each line's value.

**Labels and limits.** Models (ERA5-Land, IFS, NWM, SPoRT-LIS) use their own soils and rain. SMAP L4 is a satellite-and-model analysis whose satellite sees only the top 5 cm. Stations measure their own soil, often tens of km away. Levels often differ by 0.1 m³/m³ or more, so compare changes over the month more than levels. Percentiles rank wetness against each model's own record; they do not give an amount of water. The average is ERA5-Land's own climatology over the chosen years: it ranks this period against the model, not against the field.

**Rebuilding.** `python tools/build_moisture.py` takes about 2 minutes and writes `data/soilmoisture_stations.json` (60 KB):
- the AWDB stations (SCAN, SNTL, CSCAN, SNTLT) that report hourly SMS, with the depths of the sensors still reporting;
- the commissioned USCRN stations whose current daily file holds soil moisture in the last 60 days, with each file's name.

Readings are always read live.

## 14. The map as an image, and the Plain base

**Bases.** The layer sheet has four: Satellite (Esri World Imagery, the default, with the names overlay), USGS imagery + topo, Topographic (USGS) and Plain. There is no street base; roads and street names are the TIGER overlay, off by default.

**Plain base.** "Plain (no base map)", the last base in the layer sheet, asks for no tiles. It only marks the map (`plain-base`), and the map's background turns #fcfbf8, a shade lighter than the cover outside the chosen states. The map then holds only:
- the states' outline and cover;
- the overlay on show;
- the reference layers that are on.

Like every base except Satellite, it has no names overlay: place, river and county names go off with it and come back with Satellite. Roads, water and county lines keep their own switches, off by default. Code: `PlainBase` in `js/app.js`. It suits a clean map to read, print or save as an image.

**The map as an image.** The image button among the map tools loads `js/mapimage.js` the first time it is used (about 30 KB, no library). It draws the map, as it is on the screen, onto a canvas once for the preview, builds the footer, and opens the save dialog (§7) with both. There the size, resolution, format and the footer's texts are chosen, and the file is drawn again at that size. The image is made in the browser, and nothing is sent to a server. The steps:
1. *Waiting:* until no zoom or pan is moving and no layer is still loading (at most 8 s), then 250 ms for the tiles' fade, and until the page's fonts are ready.
2. *Size:* the map's size on the screen in CSS pixels times k, the image's pixels per CSS pixel.
   - The preview is drawn at the screen's sharpness: k = 2 on a phone (767 px wide or less), the screen's pixel ratio held between 1 and 2 on a computer.
   - The file's k is chosen in the dialog: 1, 2 (the default) or 3 times the screen; a print width of 3.5, 6.5 or 10 in, with k = inches × DPI ÷ the map's CSS width (a DPI under 300 is raised to 300 for these); or a custom width or height in pixels, inches, cm or mm. The map keeps its shape on the screen, so the height follows the width, with the footer's height (which changes as its texts are edited) added.
   - The DPI (72–1,200) is written into the file. It does not change the pixels of a size given in pixels or as a multiple of the screen; for a size in inches, cm or mm, pixels = inches × DPI.
   - k is capped so the whole image stays under about 16 million pixels on a phone (the canvas limit of some phones) and 40 million on a computer. A larger size is drawn at the cap: the size boxes show the capped size once the typing is done, and the line under them says "as large as this device can draw it".
   - At the preview's own k the preview's drawing is reused. If the map moved or was resized while the dialog was open, the preview's drawing is used at any size, and the message says so.
3. *Drawing:* the map pane's elements in the browser's painting order: negative z-index first, then 0 and auto in the page's order, then positive. Each is drawn with its opacity and CSS filter.
   - Pictures (tiles, image overlays) are drawn at their place and size, with their edges on whole pixels so neighbouring tiles meet without a seam.
   - Canvases are drawn as they are.
   - SVG (the states' outline and cover, watersheds, catchments, a drawn field, the spring-wind arrows) is serialised, cut to the part on the map, and drawn as a picture.
   - Boxes are drawn with their background colour, first linear gradient, borders, rounded corners, outer shadows and turn (the pin). Absolutely placed `::before` and `::after` are drawn too (the pin's dot).
   - Text is drawn word by word where the page laid it out.
   - A group faded as one (a tile layer at its opacity) is drawn on a layer of its own and laid on at that opacity, as the browser does, so old and new tiles that overlap don't add up.
   - A tile still fading in is drawn fully shown, as it will be a moment later.
4. *Not drawn:*
   - popups and tooltips;
   - form controls, buttons, video and frames;
   - Leaflet's own controls (zoom, attribution, scale), which sit outside the map pane; the footer gives its own scale and credits;
   - CSS beyond the above: background pictures, radial and conic gradients, inset shadows, clip paths, blend modes, text shadows.
5. *Pictures from other servers:* a canvas that holds a picture from another server can be saved only when that server allowed it with a CORS header (`Access-Control-Allow-Origin`). Otherwise the browser refuses to write the file. So the image handles such pictures this way:
   - Each one is asked for again in CORS mode, usually from the browser's cache (20 s at most).
   - If that fails, it is asked for once more with `_cors=1` added to its address, in case the cached copy was kept without the header.
   - A server that refuses both is not asked again for that image.
   - Its layer is left out, rather than leaving a blank or a file that cannot be written. It is named in the footer ("Not in this image: …") and in the message, by the app's name for the layer, else its credit, else its server's host.
   - A canvas layer is tested by drawing it onto a 1-pixel scratch canvas and reading that back. The map's own canvas is never read back, as that would make the browser draw it more slowly from then on.
   - Pictures from the app's own server, `data:` and `blob:` need no check.
   - In tests on 12 November 2026, the satellite base, the Drought Monitor, SMAP and the soil pH map all went in.
6. *Footer:* built as page elements off the screen, at the map's width (`.mi-foot` in `css/app.css`), then drawn the same way under the map, or on it where an item was placed in the dialog (below). It holds:
   - The legend on show, in full. It loses its own box, height limit and hidden rows, and a minimised legend is opened. Each choice (a list, a date, a box) is written as "label: value", and the buttons are removed. When the live legend has its % column (§15), the image's legend carries the same numbers (below).
   - The legend's key picture (the producer's GetLegendGraphic image). It gets up to 5 s more to load, then the same CORS check. If it cannot be copied, or did not load, a sentence in its place says which.
   - The app's name, with the place (county, state) and the point's coordinates (5 decimals), or else the chosen states (their number, above 3).
   - "Map made YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM (local time) · zoom z".
   - Two scale bars, metric and imperial, by Leaflet's rule: the longest round length (1, 2, 3 or 5 × 10ⁿ) that fits in max(70, min(160, 0.3 × width)) px. It is measured along the map's middle row; on Web Mercator the scale changes with latitude, so the bars are true at the middle.
   - The credits of every layer on the map (their attributions, links dropped), and the names of any layer left out.
   - A north arrow, which starts on the map at its top right. The map is Web Mercator, where north is straight up at every point, so the arrow always points up; its colours are the SVG's own, so it draws the same on any base.

   In the dialog the footer is the preview's own, made editable: an element holding only inline text (a line, a title with its unit) is one box, and other text (a legend row's label beside its colour) a box of its own. Typing changes the footer itself, so its height follows. A text emptied, or removed with its ×, is hidden, together with what held only it (a legend row's colour box, an empty line), and listed under the preview to put back. The file's footer is a copy of the edited one, without what was left out and without the editing marks.
7. *File:* PNG (with a pHYs chunk for the DPI), JPEG (its JFIF density) or TIFF (written in the app, §7), named `soilguard-map-<place>-<YYYY-MM-DD>.<png|jpg|tif>`. The place is the first part of the place's name (its county or town), else the coordinates, else the state or the number of states. It is saved through the browser's own download.

**Placing the items (the dialog's layout).** The image is the map with a white band under it. The footer's five items start in the band, laid out as the footer always was: the legend in its left column; the title (the app's name, the place, when the image was made, the zoom), the scale bars and the credits in its right column; and the north arrow on the map, 10 px in from its top and right edges. Code: `js/downloads.js` (the dialog) and `footer` in `js/mapimage.js`.
- *Moving:* each item has a ✥ handle at its top left. A mouse drags an item by its handle or by any part that is not a text (a text is typed in); a finger drags it only by its handle, so the preview still scrolls under a finger, except the north arrow, which moves by any part. With a handle focused (Tab, or after a drag), the arrow keys move its item by 1 pixel of the layout, and Shift + an arrow by 10. On a phone the handles are larger (28 px).
- *Snapping:* while dragged, an item snaps to the image's edges (10 px in), corners and middles, and to the band's top and bottom, whenever it comes within 8 screen pixels of one; a thin line in the accent colour shows where. The arrow keys never snap.
- *Where it stays:* a moved item is pinned at its place in the layout's own pixels (the map's size on the screen, plus the band). The file scales the whole layout, so the item lands at the same place at any size or DPI. It cannot leave the image, and goes into the band only while the band is on. The band grows to hold an item dropped low in it and shrinks when it holds less.
- *On the map:* an item placed on the map gets a light box behind it (white at 90%, rounded corners, a faint shadow) so it reads over imagery.
- *The Layout panel,* beside the size and format: a box for each item to show or hide it; "Show % of area for each class" (the column below); "Light box behind items on the map"; "White band under the map", which, turned off, places what the band held on the map at the first free place (the title at the top left, the legend at the bottom left, the scale bars and credits at the bottom right, else another corner, else just beside an item already placed), each a little narrower than in the band; and Reset layout, which puts every item back where it started. Reset layout keeps the texts as edited; Reset texts puts the texts back and keeps the layout.
- *Screen readers:* after each move a polite live line says where the item is, as % from the left and top, and whether it is on the map or under it.

**The % column in the image.** When the live legend shows each class's share of the area (§15), the dialog asks `WG.shares` for the same answer: at once when it is already measured, else it waits up to 2.5 s and adds the numbers to the preview's legend when they come. Each number goes at the right end of its legend row, matched by the row's colour, else by its label. A legend that is the producer's picture gets rows of its own (colour, class, %) in the picture's place, as in the live legend, and the uncoloured share is a last row with a white swatch. A line under the rows says what the numbers are of and how fine the measure was: "% of Texas, measured from the map at about 1.0 km a pixel." Turning "Show % of area for each class" off hides the numbers, the added rows and that line together, and puts a picture legend back.

## 15. Each class's share of the area (the legends' % column)

**What it shows.** The legend of every layer that colours areas gets a narrow column at the right of its classes: the share of the area in each class, in %. The column's head names the area: "% of Texas" (or of the chosen states, where more than one can be chosen), or "% of your boundary" while the area tool holds a drawn or uploaded boundary, which takes the place of the state.

A last row gives the share the map leaves uncoloured, under a name that says what no colour means on that map (table below). The classes and that row add up to 100%. While the numbers are worked out the head adds "· measuring…"; if they cannot be, it reads "% not measured" and its tooltip gives the reason. The head's tooltip otherwise says how they were measured. A new date, depth, element or boundary measures again. Code: `js/shares.js` (`WG.shares`). Each file that makes a layer says how that layer is measured (its item's `shares`: `js/app.js` for the erosion maps, `js/weather.js` for the drought maps, `js/soilmaps.js`, `js/soilmoisture.js`, `js/landcover.js`).

**Steps.**
1. *The area:* the state's outline (its file in `data/states/`), or the boundary's polygons, and the box around them (a boundary's widened by 2% on each side).
2. *The image:* one picture of the whole box, from the same service as the map, made off the screen and never shown, about 1,200 pixels on its long side: about 1 km a pixel over Texas, finer over a small boundary. It is one of:
   - a WMS GetMap, or an ArcGIS MapServer `export` or ImageServer `exportImage`, in EPSG:4326 (longitude and latitude);
   - the layer's map tiles at the lowest zoom that gives the box about 900 pixels across (at most 96 tiles, 4 at a time), put together in Web Mercator; a missing tile is clear, and more than a third missing fails the measure;
   - the statewide images the map shows already (`data/states/XX/*.png`, Web Mercator).

   A server that fails the 1,200-pixel picture is asked once more at 700; SoilGrids is asked at 800. Pictures are read as files (`fetch`, then `createImageBitmap`), never from the screen.
3. *Clipping:* the outline is drawn as a mask in the same projection and pixels, filled by the even-odd rule, so a hole in a boundary is left out and islands are in. A pixel counts when the mask covers it by half or more.
4. *The classes:* the colours to look for, and their names:
   - the legend's own colour rows (the erosion maps, pH, CEC, organic matter, salinity, sodicity, the USGS elements);
   - for a legend that is the producer's picture: the Drought Monitor's D0–D4 from the app's own table of its colours; the drought outlooks' from the service's ArcGIS legend (`legend?f=json`), each swatch read at its middle pixel;
   - for a colour scale (SMAP, SoilGrids N): the scale cut at its ticks, each colour step going to the tick range that holds its middle, labelled "< a", "a–b" and "≥ z".

   A legend row drawn clear is the uncoloured area.
5. *Matching:* each pixel inside takes the class of the nearest legend colour (straight-line distance in red, green and blue, 0–255 each) when that distance is 48 or less. For a colour scale whose neighbouring steps differ by more, the limit widens to 0.6 × that difference + 8, as a class there also holds the shades between its steps. A pixel less than half opaque is uncoloured. A pixel that matches no colour (a smoothed edge between two classes, a line or label the server drew) is left out of the shares; the share of the area left out this way is named in the tooltip when it is 0.05% or more.
6. *Area weights:* each pixel counts by its share of the ground, not as 1, taken at the middle of its row. In an EPSG:4326 picture a pixel covers ground in proportion to cos(latitude); in a Web Mercator picture, which stretches both ways by 1/cos(latitude), in proportion to cos²(latitude). Counted unweighted, a pixel at the north tip of Texas (36.5° N) would weigh as much as one at the south tip (26° N), though it covers 11% less ground in EPSG:4326 and 20% less in Web Mercator.
7. *The shares:* a class's weighted pixels ÷ (all the classed and uncoloured weighted pixels) × 100, shown to one decimal; under 0.05% it shows "<0.1%", and a class with no pixel "0%".

The land cover's shares (NLCD 2021) are not measured from a picture: they are its 30 m cells counted inside the state's outline (pre-built, equal-area) or inside your boundary from MRLC's WCS, the same numbers as the Soil tab's Land cover card. That key has no uncoloured row.

**The uncoloured row's names.**

| Layer | The uncoloured row |
|---|---|
| Drought Monitor (this week, a year or five years ago, a past week); monthly and seasonal drought outlooks | No drought |
| Wind erosion susceptibility; Wind Erodibility Group; soil pH, CEC, organic matter, salinity, sodicity | No data (water, built-up or not surveyed) |
| Climatic factor C; spring wind; USGS total elements; National Water Model saturation | No data |
| SoilGrids total N | No data (water, built-up) |
| SMAP L4 surface and root zone | No data (water, or not modelled) |
| NASA SPoRT-LIS percentile | No data (water, or outside the model) |
| NASA GRACE-DA percentile | Near normal (30th–70th percentile, not coloured) |

**Kept.** An answer is kept in memory (the last 200) and in the browser for 15 days, under the layer, what it shows besides (its date, depth or element), its classes, the state or the boundary, and the app's version, so a legend shown again has its numbers at once. A layer that changes by itself (the National Water Model's latest hour) is measured anew each time and not kept between visits. One layer is measured at a time, the one asked last first; one still waiting when its legend is drawn again or taken down is dropped unmeasured. The pixel loop gives the page back every 12 ms, so the map never waits for it. A failure is not kept: the next time the legend is drawn, it is tried again.

**A check.** For the Drought Monitor of 10 November 2026 over Texas, the column read None 29.8, D0 16.4, D1 30.3, D2 16.6, D3 5.6 and D4 1.3%; NDMC's own statistics for that map (computed from its polygons) are 29.48, 16.51, 30.54, 16.66, 5.54 and 1.28%: each within 0.35 points.

**Not measured, and limits.**
- Layers that are lines or points have no column: watersheds and streams, the soil moisture stations, roads, rivers and county lines, the soil survey's map-unit lines, the spring wind's arrows.
- The share is of the map's picture, not of the producer's own data. At about 1 km a pixel over Texas, patches smaller than a pixel blur into their neighbours, and a source's own cells may be coarser than the pixel (SMAP 9 km; the USGS elements, about one site per 1,600 km²).
- Colours the legend does not list (smoothed edges, labels) are left out of the denominator, as above; the tooltip gives their share.
- Ground the producer does not cover (water, built-up land, outside a model's domain) is in the uncoloured row, not in any class.
- A legend that gives no colours to match cannot be measured, and the head says so.
