Surface layer — edit with your soil test

Sand + silt + clay = 100. OM usually 0.5–5% (up to 20). CaCO₃ 0 in acid soils, roughly 5–40% in the calcareous soils of the Plains and West (up to 80).

Edits recalculate texture class, Wind Erodibility Group and index (the survey's group stays unless your values change what the texture rules give), RWEQ erodible fraction and crust factor, the risk and the practice plan. Silt fills automatically if sand + clay are given. The edited surface layer also goes into the soil profile (Soil maps › Soil, where pH is edited and the layer's water retention comes from Saxton & Rawls 2006) and into the model files.

Wind Erodibility Groups: what the classes mean

NRCS sorts surface soils into nine Wind Erodibility Groups (WEG) by how easily they blow when bare and dry: 1, 2, 3, 4, 4L, 5, 6, 7 and 8. Group 1 is the most erodible and group 8 is not erodible. Each group has an erodibility index I: the tons of soil per acre per year a wide, bare, smooth field would lose in the climate of Garden City, Kansas. I runs from 0 to 310; groups 3, 4 and 4L share I = 86, and 4L holds calcareous (limy) loamy soils.

· For a named soil, NRCS uses the group recorded in the soil survey; the texture guide is for soils that are not identified (National Agronomy Manual, Part 502). So a soil's group can differ from what its texture suggests: Pullman clay loam is WEG 7. Rock fragments move a soil to a less erodible group: one group at 15–35% by volume, two at 35–60%, I = 0 above 60% (sands and loamy sands move less). Texture guide: USDA-NRCS National Soil Survey Handbook, Part 618 Subpart B, §618.93 (2024). “Calcareous” means the soil fizzes strongly in dilute acid (free lime).

Units

Numbers on screen, the charts and the CSV downloads of charts and tables follow this table, and your choice is remembered on this device. Two CSV files keep fixed units, named in their columns: the soil profile from the Soil maps › Soil tab (cm, cm/h) and the area analysis (acres, t/ac/yr). Model files (DSSAT, SWAT+, EPIC, APEX, APSIM, AquaCrop) always use each model's own units. Erodibility I is an NRCS figure in tons per acre per year, and the risk classes are this app's own, set in the same unit. Practice specifications come from Texas NRCS standards and keep the original in brackets when converted.
